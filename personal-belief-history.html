Personal Belief History

A reconstruction log for what I actually believed at different stages of my life. This page is deliberately separate from the scholarly research corpus.

Rule: unknown stays unknown. Baptism, church participation, Christian language, or present-day beliefs are not enough to backfill what I thought years earlier.

Purpose

Reconstruct earlier beliefs without rewriting the past from my current perspective. This is a personal evidence record, not evidence for or against Christianity or any other religion.

What is currently established

  • I was baptized at about 16, based on present recollection; no contemporaneous record has been checked yet.
  • I do not clearly remember how I understood Christianity during adolescence or college.
  • My current framing of wrongdoing is closer to “I do things that are wrong sometimes” than “I am a sinner” as a permanent identity or human condition.
  • That current framing must not be projected backward onto teenage or college me without evidence.

Evidence rules

  1. Contemporaneous evidence: journals, messages, emails, social posts, school writing, church/baptism material, notes, recordings.
  2. Near-contemporaneous evidence: material created close to the period being reconstructed.
  3. Present recollection: useful but vulnerable to memory distortion and later reinterpretation.
  4. Inference: label it explicitly; never turn it into a remembered fact.

Participation is not doctrine. Baptism, church attendance, prayer, or calling myself Christian may show religious identification or practice, but they do not establish what I believed about any specific doctrine.

Questions to reconstruct rather than assume

For each period, look for what I actually thought about God; Jesus; the Trinity and Holy Spirit; biblical authority; sin and human nature; salvation and atonement; heaven, hell and judgment; morality; science and history; gender and sexuality; church authority; prayer and miracles; and any doubts I had at the time.

Evidence to look for

Old journals, school papers, notes apps, texts, DMs, emails, social-media posts, church or baptism records, Bible annotations, photos/captions, and written conversations.

For each recovered item, record the date, source, exact claim, confidence, and whether it reflects belief, uncertainty, social conformity, or merely language I was using.

Do not backfill the story

The goal is not to create a neat belief or deconversion narrative after the fact. Preserve the difference between what I know I believed, what I currently remember believing, what evidence suggests, and what I simply do not know.

A gap is better than a false memory.
