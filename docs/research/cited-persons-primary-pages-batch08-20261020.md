## 66. Philostorgius

**Current unmodified claim:** ⟨UNRESOLVED⟩ **Philostorgius** — Arian-sympathetic church historian of Nicaea. Survives only as a hostile summary in Photius's later digest — the dissenting side at Nicaea reaches us through its enemies' hands. The entry currently has no directly linked evidence sufficient to support its full wording. Retain it only as an audit lead, not as an established fact. `UNRESOLVED 12 AUG 2026 — DIRECT SOURCE OR PAGE REQUIRED`

**Verdict:** CORRECT major source-survival and chronology errors; firsthand *Photius-transmitted epitome* accessed.

**Evidence and exact textual locator:** Photius's epitome of Philostorgius, *Ecclesiastical History*, Book I chs. 7–9 (Edward Walford trans., 1855), recounts the Council of Nicaea (325). The translated *Bibliotheca* codex 40 notice explicitly condemns Philostorgius' Eunomian/Arian sympathies. Philostorgius was a **fifth-century historian writing retrospectively**, not a contemporary eyewitness at Nicaea.

**Sources:** https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/philostorgius.htm ; https://www.tertullian.org/rpearse/philostorgius_epitome.html

**Counterevidence / limitation:** The original twelve-volume Greek work is lost as a whole, but **not solely preserved via Photius**: *Passion of Artemius*, the Suda and other fragment witnesses also preserve passages. Scholarly reconstructed edition (Bidez/Winkelmann), and Philip R. Amidon 2007, distinguish epitome from independently preserved fragments. https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/philostorgius

**Proposed source-bounded wording (NOT YET PUBLISHED):** 'Philostorgius was a fifth-century pro-Eunomian church historian whose retrospective account of the fourth-century controversies, including Nicaea, survives mainly in Photius's hostile ninth-century epitome **and in other fragments**. It supplies a mediated partisan perspective rather than independent eyewitness testimony.'

## 67. Photius

**Current unmodified claim:** ⟨UNRESOLVED⟩ **Photius** (Patriarch) — Summarized Philostorgius; the sole surviving conduit for the Arian account of Nicaea. The entry currently has no directly linked evidence sufficient to support its full wording. Retain it only as an audit lead, not as an established fact. `UNRESOLVED 12 AUG 2026 — DIRECT SOURCE OR PAGE REQUIRED`

**Verdict:** CORRECT 'sole surviving conduit' false; firsthand translated *Bibliotheca* notice and epitome accessible.

**Evidence and exact textual locator:** Photius, *Bibliotheca*, codex 40 (quoted in Walford 1855 translation), describes the twelve books and condemns their pro-Arian rhetoric. Photius's epitome transmits Book I chs. 7–9 concerning Nicaea. Walford intro acknowledges both epitome and *Bibliotheca* notice.

**Sources:** https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/philostorgius.htm ; https://kfhist.awk.nrw.de/text/E7

**Counterevidence / limitation:** Other indirect fragment witnesses, including *Passio Artemii*, exist, so 'sole' is not defensible. More than a conduit, Photius selected, abridged and condemned material; hostile editorial mediation affects reconstruction. This is an epistemic limitation, not license to presume Philostorgius' claims historically correct.

**Proposed source-bounded wording (NOT YET PUBLISHED):** 'Ninth-century patriarch Photius wrote a hostile notice of Philostorgius (*Bibliotheca* 40) and an epitome of his lost church history. That epitome is its main surviving witness, supplemented by extracts preserved elsewhere.'

**Current unmodified claim:** ⟨UNRESOLVED⟩ **Popper, Karl** — Philosopher of science; *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1934/1959). Falsifiability as a demarcation criterion, not a truth test — an unfalsifiable claim is not thereby false but carries no evidential weight in either direction. Retain it only as an audit lead, not as an established fact. `UNRESOLVED 12 AUG 2026 — DIRECT SOURCE OR PAGE REQUIRED`

**Verdict:** HOLDS demarcation, CORRECT excess 'no evidential weight in either direction'.

**Evidence and exact textual locator:** Popper, *Logik der Forschung* (issued 1934 with 1935 imprint); heavily revised English *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* **(1959)**. First original chapter excerpts at 1959 pp.27–34 explain why accumulated observations cannot inductively *prove* a universal statement. An ISSR scholarly introduction to 1959 edition confirms Popper's purpose was **demarcation** rather than a truth test.

**Sources:** https://www.issr.org.uk/library/the-logic-of-scientific-discovery/ ; https://www.peterharrington.co.uk/the-logic-of-scientific-discovery-174504.html ; https://studylib.net/doc/7677979/karl-popper---american-university-of-beirut

**Counterevidence / limitation:** Popper did **not** simply declare metaphysical/unfalsifiable ideas false or meaningless; nor does non-falsifiability by itself entail 'no evidential weight in either direction.' Historicity, philosophical and metaphysical claims can be discussed with evidence outside this one demarcation test. Don't treat falsifiability as an exclusive or universally adopted philosophy-of-science rule.

**Proposed source-bounded wording (NOT YET PUBLISHED):** 'Philosopher Karl Popper's *Logik der Forschung* (1934; revised English *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*, 1959) defended falsifiability as a way of distinguishing empirical scientific theories from other claims. It is a proposed demarcation criterion, not a direct test of truth, meaningfulness or every kind of evidential support.'
