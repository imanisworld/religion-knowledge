**Scope:** Maximus the Confessor; James Luther Mays; Amihai Mazar; Sean McDowell; Lee Martin McDonald. **Record type:** research-only follow-up to [tracker #198](https://github.com/imanisworld/religion-knowledge/issues/198) and the first three batches in [PR #216](https://github.com/imanisworld/religion-knowledge/pull/216). **At this check, canonical `main` still had 194 DOCUMENTED / 171 UNRESOLVED of 365.** All five remain `UNRESOLVED` for publication. The tests below distinguish primary evidence **of a person's own position**, near-contemporary testimony **about a historical person**, bibliographic proof **a work exists**, later theological tradition, and independent verification that a claim is true. Web result snippets are not treated as page-level examinations.

## Source-by-source audit

### 1. Maximus the Confessor

- **Existing exact claim:** “(c. 580–662) — Byzantine theologian. Opposed Monothelitism. Tongue excised and right hand severed (661); exiled to Lazica; died 13 August 662; vindicated posthumously at the Third Council of Constantinople (680–681).”
- **Verdict: CORRECT (date) + QUALIFY (council).** The entry's **661 mutilation date** is unreliable: scholarly accounts place the final condemnation and mutilation in **662**. The **Sixth Ecumenical Council (Third of Constantinople, 680–681)** formally upheld the dyothelite doctrine he defended; scholarly treatment notes it **did not name Maximus**. “Vindicated posthumously” must be expressly **doctrinal**, not personal or a retroactive institutional reversal of his sentence.
- **Ancient primary-document lead — NOT personally checked at manuscript/translation passage level:** the trial/exile dossier, especially `DB` (Dispute at Bizya), Anastasius the Apocrisiarius' letter reporting exile and `Vita Maximi`, translated in Pauline Allen and Bronwen Neil (eds./trans.), *Maximus the Confessor and His Companions: Documents from Exile* (Oxford, 2002). [Hosted introductory discussion](https://www.scribd.com/document/542087033/ALLEN-P-NEIL-B-Eds-Maximus-the-Confessor-and-His-Companions) identifies *Vita Maximi* **PG 90:104B–105C**, *DB* §17, *Commentary* §4, and *Ep. Anas.* §§1, 4–5 in connection with mutilation/exile/death; **this is a secondary/editorial introduction, not the original deposition directly checked**.
- **Independent scholarly source checked:** Pauline Allen, [“Life and Times of Maximus the Confessor” (2015), *Oxford Handbook*, **pp. 3–18**](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordhb/9780199673834.013.1), abstract affirms death **13 August 662** (full chapter restricted). Andrew Louth, *Maximus the Confessor* (Routledge, 1996), **introductory biographical discussion in a hosted preview**, says his tongue and right hand were cut off and he died in Lazica **13 August 662**; specifies that **the council of 680 did not mention him by name**; [later secondary synthesis (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1002/9780470670606.wbecc0873) explicitly distinguishes tradition from certain biographical details. [2008 papal audience](https://www.vatican.va/content/benedict-xvi/en/audiences/2008/documents/hf_ben-xvi_aud_20080625.html) reiterates the date, but is **religious retrospective testimony**, not an ancient eyewitness. [Old *Lives of Saints* account](https://www.copticplace.org/Saints_E/Lives_of_Saints/Maximus.html), closing paragraphs, gives the condemnation and 662 exile; **late devotional retelling**, not first-hand seventh-century record.
- **Counterevidence/uncertainty:** hagiographic versions include miracle narratives; exact journey details and early biographies are contested. **No original ancient page was directly opened in this pass**, so retain source caveat.
- **Publication-safe proposed wording:** “Maximus the Confessor (c. 580–662) opposed the doctrine that Christ had only one will. Accounts of his imperial persecution report the cutting of his tongue and right hand in **662**, followed by exile to Lazica, where he died on **13 August 662**. The Third Council of Constantinople (680–681) later endorsed the two-wills doctrine he defended, though the council did not rehabilitate him by name.”
- **Confidence:** high for later historiographic date/doctrinal outcome; **medium** until ancient trial documents are read; **action:** correct 661→662 in a future synchronized publication PR, then inspect Allen/Neil's documentary locators before full promotion.

### 2. James Luther Mays

- **Existing exact claim:** “Critical scholar; *Amos* (OT Library, 1969); *Micah* (OT Library, 1976).”
- **Verdict: HOLDS for verified bibliography.** The current entry does not attribute an interpretive conclusion; publication records and original book front matter suffice to check the narrow work/year statements.
- **Original texts directly visible:** *Micah: A Commentary*, [licensed embedded original book preview](https://biblia.com/api/plugins/embeddedpreview?historybuttons=false&layout=minimal&navigationbox=false&resourceName=LLS%3AOTL33MIC&sharebutton=false), **title/copyright page and table of contents/preface** showing “© 1976 James Luther Mays” and Old Testament Library. Its headings include **Micah 6:6–8**, but **the preview does not expose the body commentary**; do not infer his interpretive conclusion from a heading. For *Amos*, [original 1969 book metadata](https://books.google.com/books?id=EdvYAAAAMAAJ) and [publisher's current catalog](https://www.wjkbooks.com/bookproduct/0664224423-amos/) confirm the series, author, and year. The *Micah* [1976 WorldCat record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/2020740) independently corroborates.
- **Independent context:** Mays was a recognized critical Old Testament specialist, but “critical” denotes academic biblical exegesis and need not imply his attitude to Christian faith. No position on Amos 5 or Micah 6 is proved merely by the titles.
- **Publication-safe proposed wording:** “James Luther Mays was an Old Testament scholar who wrote *Amos: A Commentary* (Old Testament Library, 1969) and *Micah: A Commentary* (Old Testament Library, 1976). Both titles and original publication dates are independently confirmed; any specific exegesis attributed to him must cite its own passage.”
- **Confidence:** **high** for bibliographic attributions; **action:** narrow DOCUMENTED promotion can rely on original book front matter plus publisher/library records, without asserting unread interpretations.

### 3. Amihai Mazar

- **Existing exact claim:** “Critical archaeologist; ‘modified conventional chronology.’ Middle position on the scale of David's kingdom.”
- **Verdict: HOLDS, but replace imprecise “middle position” with Mazar's actual data and interpretation.** His archaeological periodization is a *proposed dating system*, not proof of the biblical United Monarchy.
- **Original publication PERSONALLY read, with page-image inspection:** Amihai Mazar, [“The Debate over the Chronology of the Iron Age in the Southern Levant: Its History, the Current Situation, and a Suggested Resolution,” in T. Levy and T. Higham (eds.), *The Bible and Radiocarbon Dating* (2005), **pp. 15–30**](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9781317491514_A23898016/preview-9781317491514_A23898016.pdf). **Printed p. 21 (PDF page index 33)** states his “Modified Conventional Chronology” places the Iron I/II boundary near **980 BCE** and end of Iron IIA near **840/830 BCE**; it also admits many contexts cannot decisively be assigned to 10th or 9th century. **Printed p. 26 (PDF page index 38)** characterizes David/Solomon's historical polity as a **modest geographically limited development**, says the evidence does *not* require a large empire, and warns that archaeology cannot closely verify the biblical narrative. [University research bibliography](https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/the-debate-over-the-chronology-of-the-iron-age-in-the-southern-le/) independently confirms authorship/pages.
- **Competing scholarship:** Israel Finkelstein, [“The Iron Age Chronology Debate: Is the Gap Narrowing?” *Near Eastern Archaeology* **74.1** (2011): **50–54**](https://israelfinkelstein.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/07/gap-narrowing-nea.pdf), distinguishes the Low Chronology (**~930/920 BCE** transition) from Mazar's proposed **~980 BCE**, discusses date-calibration disagreements, and explicitly notes convergence on some chronologic limits. This critique does not automatically refute the entire MCC.
- **Publication-safe proposed wording:** “Archaeologist Amihai Mazar proposed a ‘Modified Conventional Chronology’ for the early Iron Age, dating Iron IIA approximately **980–840/830 BCE** (2005, p. 21). He argues for a more limited Davidic-Solomonic polity than the expansive biblical picture, while acknowledging that archaeology cannot confidently tie many Iron IIA structures to either the tenth or ninth centuries (pp. 21, 26). His chronology remains disputed.”
- **Confidence:** **high** as his personally inspected attribution, **contested** as independent archaeological chronology and extent of early monarchy. **Action:** suitable for narrow source-checked promotion after main/source/reader/search/test integration.

### 4. Sean McDowell

- **Existing exact claim:** “Popular evangelical apologist.”
- **Verdict: HOLDS, PRECISION IMPROVES.** “Popular” is vague and subjective; the precise verifiable role and Christian apologetic position are more useful.
- **Author and institutional first-party sources PERSONALLY checked:** [Sean McDowell biography](https://seanmcdowell.org/author/bio) describes his apologetics doctorate, his work defending Christianity, and *The Fate of the Apostles*. [Sean McDowell, “What's Ahead,” **3 January 2026**](https://seanmcdowell.org/blog/whats-ahead), confirms first-person his teaching and apologetics work. [Biola University's faculty profile](https://www.biola.edu/directory/people/sean-mcdowell) identifies him as **Associate Professor of Christian Apologetics**, PhD from Southern Baptist Theological Seminary, and multiple relevant books. A [2023 Biola faculty interview](https://www.biola.edu/blogs/talbot-magazine/2023/faculty-profile-sean-mcdowell) records his own description of teaching classes on the problem of evil, resurrection and apologetics.
- **Independent/ideological caution:** his self-description explicitly aims to defend Christian faith; that properly contextualizes his work as *Christian apologetics*. His employment at an evangelical university is not by itself evidence that *every particular argument* he advances is evangelical, correct, or incorrect. If this entry later cites a historical claim such as fate of apostles, separately audit an actual text passage.
- **Publication-safe proposed wording:** “Sean McDowell is a Christian apologist and associate professor of Christian Apologetics at Biola University's Talbot School of Theology. His authored/coauthored works include *The Fate of the Apostles* and *Evidence That Demands a Verdict* (with Josh McDowell). His stated professional work includes defending Christian claims about Jesus, the resurrection and morality.”
- **Confidence:** **high** for role/author and attributed apologetic goals. **Action:** narrow DOCUMENTED promotion supported by his own writings and institutional confirmation.

### 5. Lee Martin McDonald

- **Existing exact claim:** “*The Biblical Canon* (2007). Scholarly treatment of canon formation.”
- **Verdict: HOLDS for bibliography and subject; author's chapter-level theses are not checked.**
- **Original book bibliographic record PERSONALLY checked; book text itself NOT read:** Lee Martin McDonald, [*The Biblical Canon: Its Origin, Transmission, and Authority* (Hendrickson, **2007**, **546 pp.**, ISBN 9781565639256)](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Biblical_Canon.html?id=r9FUWj_w9-YC). Publisher book jacket, reproduced there, identifies it as an introduction to Jewish and Christian biblical canons and calls it a third expanded revised edition of earlier *The Formation of the Christian Biblical Canon*. [Open Library edition record](https://openlibrary.org/books/OL8665989M/The_Biblical_Canon) independently confirms the edition/year.
- **Independent disciplinary evaluation:** an [SBL *Review of Biblical Literature* review (Oct. 2024)](https://www.sblcentral.org/API/Reviews/1002031_73209.pdf) discusses his **2017** two-volume *The Formation of the Biblical Canon*, specifically noting it as a later fourth edition of the earlier one-volume book and separating editions. This review is **not** a page-by-page validation of the 2007 arguments.
- **Publication-safe proposed wording:** “Biblical canon scholar Lee Martin McDonald authored *The Biblical Canon: Its Origin, Transmission, and Authority* (Hendrickson, 2007), an expanded edition of his work on how biblical texts came to be recognized as authoritative. The publication and subject are verified; exact positions on Marcion, dates and closure of the canon require passage-specific citations.”
- **Confidence:** **high** bibliographic/subject, **not yet checked** interpretive details. **Action:** narrow bibliographic DOCUMENTED upgrade possible, without pretending to have read specific 2007 pages; defer interpretation review.
