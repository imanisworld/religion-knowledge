## Source-backed findings

### K'iche' Maya — Popol Vuh manuscript

- **Repository record:** `trad_kiche_maya_religion`. The currently recorded 1701–1703 manuscript window needs precision review.
- **Direct source:** [Newberry digital collections, Ayer MS 1515](https://collections.newberry.org/archive/-2KXJ8ZSJPW6AI.html) catalogs the Ximénez manuscript as *probably written between 1700 and 1703*.
- **Dating qualification:** [Newberry's Popol Vuh guide](https://www.newberry.org/collection/research-guide/popol-vuh) gives a wider **1700–1715** copying interval. The [Library of Congress bibliographic record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2021668226/) repeats the **1700–1703** metadata, without offering independent manuscript dating.
- **Scope:** These concern a surviving K'iche'/Spanish transcription, not the first composition or origin of K'iche' religious practice. The lost earlier version is only *probably* sixteenth-century in Newberry's guide; the repository's exact 1554–1558 composition estimate has not been rechecked against the cited scholarly pages in this batch.
- **Next source gate:** Consult the relevant published passages from Tedlock/Recinos and an independently reviewed manuscript chronology before changing that precise composition range.

### Late Bronze Age Canaanite religious world — Ugarit

- **Repository record:** `trad_late_bronze_canaanite_religion`.
- **Direct source:** [Louvre object AO 16643 / RS 3.361](https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010141446): an excavated Ras Shamra cuneiform tablet, cataloged as a fragment of the **Baal cycle**, in the **1400–1200 BCE** Late Bronze II horizon, found in 1931. The Louvre object-level page cites KTU 1.1.
- **Scope:** Strong direct support for one Ugaritic object and its museum dating. It does not date the beginnings of Canaanite religion, establish continuous practice outside Ugarit, or demonstrate borrowing into early Israelite religion.
- **Next source gate:** Compare source edition KTU 1.1 and a peer-reviewed historical account for a wider regional summary. Preserve distinctions among Canaanite religious sites.

## Next research batch: four additional traditions

### Aksumite state religion before Christianity — Endubis coin

- **Evidence:** [British Museum, Endubis silver coin 1989,0518.6](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1989-0518-6), museum-dated c. **270–300 CE** and described as showing a disc-and-crescent motif on both sides.
- **Access level:** Only a sufficiently detailed indexed **museum search result** was accessible; the object webpage itself returned HTTP **403**. Recorded with \`SEARCH_SNIPPET\`, not as a direct read of the coin or a numismatic catalogue.
- **What follows:** One early Aksumite official symbol; this alone cannot identify Mahrem/Astar/Beher-Meder, a "first" religion, or a continuity line. The specific pre-Christian Ezana inscriptions (RIÉ 185 / DAE 6–7) remain unchecked at passage level.

### Aksumite Christianity — an imperial letter and a cross-bearing coin

- **Text source:** [Athanasius, *Apologia ad Constantium* §31](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2813.htm) reproduces a letter of Constantius to Aezanes and Sazanes, referring to **their churches** and directing that Bishop Frumentius be sent to George of Alexandria. **Directly read in English translation**; Greek/Latin critical edition not checked. This witnesses an organized Christian episcopate and imperial intervention, while proving neither the precise conversion date nor the ruler's compliance.
- **Object source:** [British Museum anonymous coin 1989,0518.87](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/C_1989-0518-87), bearing a Greek cross and catalogued **c. 340–540 CE**. Museum indexed result only; the object page returned HTTP 403. The coin is **anonymous** and cannot date **Ezana's** conversion or even be securely attributed to him.
- **Further interpretation, separately:** [The Met, *African Christianity in Ethiopia*](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/african-christianity-in-ethiopia) places Ezana's adoption of Christianity in his fourth-century reign, drawing on Rufinus and Socrates Scholasticus. This secondary synthesis has not been made an exact-year primary-source claim.

### Kongo Christianity — Afonso I's dated 1517 request

- **Evidence locator:** [The Met's curatorial article, 30 Oct 2015](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2015/kongo/blog/posts/kongo-christian-art) identifies the **8 June 1517** letter from Afonso I to Manuel I, held in Portugal's **Arquivo Nacional da Torre do Tombo**, asking for devotional items including a silver cross and altarpieces.
- **Access level:** **Direct reading of the museum's secondary description** of an identified primary document; no manuscript transcription or Portuguese archival facsimile checked.
- **Limits:** A royal request supports patronage and exchange, but does not show delivery, full-population conversion or the replacement of Kongo ritual practices. The Met's object-level catalog also dates identifiable Kongo crucifixes to the **sixteenth–seventeenth centuries**; those objects should not be mistaken for the exact items requested in 1517.

### Antonian movement — Beatriz Kimpa Vita, 1704–1706

- **Evidence:** [The Met, Alexander Ives Bortolot (2003)](https://www.metmuseum.org/ko/essays/dona-beatriz-kongo-prophet) directly describes the **1704** possession claim, the localization of Christian sacred geography in Kongo, demands for political reunification and the execution of Kimpa Vita in **1706**. [Cambridge UP's description of John Thornton's 1998 monograph](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/kongolese-saint-anthony/C4CA5BBE5BAB38F5120CF2E510D8BCF7) is accessible in search results, not the whole book.
- **Source problem:** No original Italian **Capuchin testimony** was read. The movement's opponents shaped much of the surviving documentary record; reconstructing the participant's own voice from those accounts remains interpretive.
- **Detected disagreement:** The Met's **2003** essay names **Pedro II** as ordering the 1706 execution; its **2026** essay instead names **Pedro IV**. This apparent discrepancy requires a documented correction/check against the historical record. The new claim records Kimpa Vita's execution **without** attaching an unverified ruler's name. The exact political responsibility remains unresolved.
- **Material evidence caution:** [Met pendant 1999.295.1](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/318317) illustrates devotion to St. Anthony but is catalogued broadly **sixteenth–nineteenth century**; it cannot automatically be assigned to movement members or the 1704–1706 years.

**Method note:** All added Claims are \`draft\`. Directly read translated primary texts, directly read museum curator pages, and inaccessible museum records found via indexed search carry **different verification-method labels**. A bibliographic catalogue does not establish the underlying religious doctrine by itself.

## Evidence distinctions for all 27 Traditions

The public catalog distinguishes **documented period**, **surviving manuscript**, **archaeological context**, **estimated composition**, **oral origin**, and **internal religious origin claims**. Every additional statement about named beliefs, practice, historical changes, and community continuity requires a directly checked source locator.
