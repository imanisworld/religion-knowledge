**Verification standard:** “Primary” below means a surviving text authored by the named person, or for persons whose writings are lost, testimony of an ancient witness (explicitly labeled **hostile**, **hagiographic**, or indirect). ## Decision matrix: exact earlier attributions, corrected wording, page/section and counterevidence

### 1. Marcella of Rome

- **Exact existing claim:** “Biblical scholar and ascetic; part of the Aventine circle with Paula and Jerome.”
- **Verdict:** **HOLDS WITH QUALIFICATION**. Primary fourth/fifth-century testimony directly supports a Roman ascetic engaged in unusually rigorous scriptural argument; the tight circle is documented. Claims about her thoughts must acknowledge Jerome's biased eulogistic portrayal.
- **Primary passage checked:** Jerome, **Letter 127 to Principia (412 CE), §§5–7** ([text, NPNF translation](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3001127.htm)). **§5**: Jerome names Paula, describes her friendship with Marcella and Eustochium's training in Marcella's house. **§7**: he recalls Marcella questioning and disputing his scriptural exegesis; after his departure, others sought her resolution of scriptural controversies. **§§9–11** describe her organized resistance to teaching associated with Origen/Rufinus: partisan evidence, not independent proof of the heresy accusations.
- **Independent study:** Michael Graves, “The Biblical Scholarship of a Fourth-Century Woman: Marcella of Rome,” *Ephemerides Theologicae Lovanienses* **87.4 (2011): 375–391**, DOI [10.2143/ETL.87.4.2149594](https://poj.peeters-leuven.be/content.php?id=2149594&journal_code=ETL&url=article), **journal abstract checked; article pages not checked**. Graves argues that Jerome's portrayals suggest real biblical-philological activity while acknowledging their selectivity.
- **Counterevidence/uncertainty:** Most surviving narrative is Jerome's praise of an ally, and Marcella's independent voice is thinly preserved. “Aventine circle” is modern shorthand for networks rather than a formal academy; do not imply a documented membership register.
- **Safe proposed wording:** “Marcella of Rome was a late-fourth-century Christian ascetic and scriptural interlocutor in Jerome's Roman network, associated with Paula and Eustochium. Jerome's *Letter 127* (§§5–7) describes her vigorous questioning of his interpretations and influence on the Roman group; the account is also a eulogy reflecting Jerome's theological alliances.”
- **Confidence:** **High for Jerome's direct attestation**, **medium for reconstructing Marcella's independent scholarly role**. **Action:** eligible for a qualified attribution promotion after synchronized publication checks.

### 2. Marcion

- **Exact existing claim:** “(c. 85–160 CE) — Proposed the first known Christian canon: reduced Luke plus ten Pauline letters, no Old Testament. Excommunicated c. 144. Looked at the Hebrew Bible's God and Jesus's God and said they cannot be the same — his answer was two divine beings. His challenge probably accelerated canon list-making.”
- **Verdict:** **OVERSTATED / PARTIALLY HOLDS**. Ancient sources attest a Marcion-associated gospel and Pauline letter collection and a creator/Christ-Father contrast. Calling it *the first canon* cannot prove there were no earlier collections. Calling his gospel “reduced Luke” adopts his opponents' contested directional relationship, while a precise excommunication year and his causal role in the larger canon process require separate evidence.
- **Primary ancient hostile witnesses personally checked:** Tertullian, *Adversus Marcionem* **4.1–2** ([E. Evans translation](https://tertullian.org/articles/evans_marc/evans_marc_10book4_eng.htm)); **4.1** characterizes the *Antitheses* as distinguishing the God of the Law from the God of the gospel; **4.2** calls Marcion's gospel derivative of Luke. Tertullian, *Adversus Marcionem* **5.1 and 5.21** ([text/translation](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/03125.htm)): specifically disputes Marcion's appropriation of Paul's letters, includes Philemon, and notes the exclusion of the Pastorals (1–2 Timothy, Titus). Neither author nor ancient source establishes via direct extant Marcion writing that Marcion himself used “canon” in our later technical sense.
- **Independent scholarship:** Dieter T. Roth, “Prophets, Priests, and Kings: Old Testament Figures in Marcion's Gospel and Luke,” in *Connecting Gospels: Beyond the Canonical/Non-Canonical Divide* (2018), **pp. 41–56**, [Oxford abstract](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198814801.003.0003) **read at abstract level only**. Roth notes that elements of Marcion's gospel remain **unattested** in the hostile witnesses, blocking blanket claims about its contents. [Ernest Evans, *Adversus Marcionem*, introduction](https://www.tertullian.org/articles/evans_marc/evans_marc_02intro.htm) discusses the collection and the limits of the claim that Marcion caused subsequent canon formation; Evans notes ancient hostile accounts dominate the record.
- **Counterevidence/uncertainty:** No complete original Marcionite gospel/letter codex survives; the relationship between Marcion's gospel and canonical Luke, the dating/meaning of the Rome separation (often **c. 144**), and **causal** claim that he accelerated later canon lists require explicit qualified treatment. The birth/death years **c. 85–160** are approximations; omit if not source-checked.
- **Safe proposed wording:** “Marcion of Sinope, a second-century Christian teacher, is associated by later opponents with an early bounded collection of Christian scripture: a gospel closely related to Luke and ten Pauline letters, excluding the Jewish scriptures as authority. Tertullian reports that Marcion distinguished the creator depicted in Jewish scripture from the higher Father of Jesus. Surviving accounts are mainly polemical, and both the gospel's textual direction of dependence and Marcion's influence on wider canon formation remain debated.”
- **Confidence:** **High** for what Tertullian *reports*, **medium** for broad collection profile, **low** for priority, dating and causality. **Action:** do not promote the complete old attribution without replacing overconfident pieces; optional narrower verified attribution by synchronized PR.

### 3. Martin Luther King Jr.

- **Exact existing claim:** “Cited Amos 5:24 at the March on Washington (August 28, 1963) and in ‘Letter from Birmingham Jail’ (April 1963). Treated as a confirmed primary source for the verse's reception history.”
- **Verdict:** **HOLDS.** Both uses are demonstrably present; this documents a twentieth-century application of Amos, not proof of Amos's original intention.
- **Primary texts read (modern transcripts):** King's **“I Have a Dream”**, delivered **28 Aug 1963**, [NAACP transcript](https://naacp.org/find-resources/history-explained/1963-march-washington), paragraph opening **“No! no, we are not satisfied”**, invoking justice like waters and righteousness like a mighty stream; [Yale Avalon transcription](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/mlk01.asp) cross-check. King, **“Letter from Birmingham Jail”**, dated **16 Apr 1963**, [full text transcription](https://letterfromjail.com/), paragraph beginning **“Was not Jesus an extremist”**, explicitly names Amos as an extremist for justice and quotes the same verse. These web transcriptions are **not manuscript facsimiles**.
- **Independent institutional context:** [Martin Luther King Jr. Research and Education Institute, Stanford, “Letter from Birmingham Jail”](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/letter-birmingham-jail), publication history identifies **1963** periodical and pamphlet printings and King's **1964** revision in *Why We Can't Wait*; [Stanford speech context](https://kinginstitute.stanford.edu/i-have-dream) confirms the **28 August** address. Differences between periodical printings or full spoken recordings and online reprints do **not** affect the presence of this Amos sentence, which independently appears in multiple reproductions.
- **Counterevidence/uncertainty:** Biblical translations vary in “waters/water”, “ever-flowing/mighty stream”, and punctuation; avoid claiming that King's quotation is verbatim from one specific biblical translation without verifying that translation and edition. Do not infer King's full interpretation of Amos 5:21–24 from this one reused line.
- **Safe proposed wording:** “In ‘Letter from Birmingham Jail’ (16 April 1963), Martin Luther King Jr. invoked Amos 5:24 as an example of justice-minded ‘extremism.’ In his ‘I Have a Dream’ speech at the March on Washington (28 August 1963), he invoked the same verse while insisting that the fight for civil rights was unfinished. These are direct, independently checkable examples of Amos's modern reception.”
- **Confidence:** **High**. **Action:** **eligible for DOCUMENTED** after synced source/reader/search/tests promotion; no general historical claim about biblical Amos established thereby.

### 4. J. Louis Martyn

- **Exact existing claim:** “Critical scholar; *History and Theology in the Fourth Gospel* (1968); *Galatians* (Anchor Bible, 1997). Two-level-drama reconstruction of the *aposynagogos* — now a contested hypothesis rather than settled background. Apocalyptic-Paul framework.”
- **Verdict:** **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, ORIGINAL PAGE CHECK MISSING**. Bibliography and detailed attributed two-level hypothesis confirmed from serious secondary analyses; Martyn's own *1968/1979/2003* book pages were **not personally accessible/read**, and the broad “apocalyptic Paul framework” needs a specific Martyn-authored page.
- **Primary-book bibliographic lead only:** [Google Books, *History and Theology in the Fourth Gospel* (Harper & Row, 1968)](https://books.google.com/books/about/History_and_Theology_in_the_Fourth_Gospe.html?id=ruDYAAAAMAAJ), including chapter headings (“A Blind Beggar Receives His Sight” and “He Is Excluded from the Synagogue and Enters the Church”); [revised 1979 edition](https://books.google.com/books?id=4eDYAAAAMAAJ). **No author passage opened**: do not falsely call these page-level checks.
- **Independent scholarly source with precise claim:** Martinus C. de Boer, [“Expulsion from the Synagogue: J. L. Martyn's *History and Theology in the Fourth Gospel* Revisited,” *New Testament Studies* **66.3** (2020): **367–391**, abstract](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0028688519000535), accurately distinguishes Martyn's posited **first** trauma of exclusion after messianic confession from the **second** of blasphemy-associated violence after high Christology. Jonathan Bernier, [“Rethinking the Historicity of the Johannine Expulsion Passages” (2014)](https://bibleinterp.arizona.edu/articles/2014/06/ber388011), specifically discusses John **9:22, 12:42, 16:2** and the contested *Birkat ha-Minim* mechanism.
- **Counterevidence:** Tobias Hägerland, [“John's Gospel: A Two-Level Drama?” *Journal for the Study of the New Testament* **25.3** (2003), journal abstract](https://doi.org/10.1177/0142064X0302500304), concludes the method lacks persuasive ancient generic and internal textual evidence; de Boer (2020) is more receptive to the hypothesis but revises the proposed expulsion trigger. Differing stances make presenting a historical Johannine mass expulsion as *fact* unsafe.
- **Safe proposed wording (still a held proposal):** “J. Louis Martyn's *History and Theology in the Fourth Gospel* (1968; revised editions 1979 and 2003) advances the influential, disputed ‘two-level drama’ reading: episodes about Jesus, especially the synagogue-exclusion passages in John 9:22, 12:42 and 16:2, also reflect conflicts he reconstructs for the Gospel's later community. Scholars disagree over whether that second historical level and its proposed expulsion mechanism can be demonstrated. His interpretation of Paul in *Galatians* (1997) needs a separately checked passage before specifying its framework.”
- **Confidence:** **Medium** as scholarly attribution via detailed criticism, **low/undetermined** for Martyn's precise uninspected words. **Action:** remain **UNRESOLVED** until original author pages are checked for both works or split the entry into properly sourced narrow claims.

### 5. Steve Mason

- **Exact existing claim:** “Critical scholar. Argues ‘Judeans’ is the strongest translation specifically for Josephus.”
- **Verdict:** **HOLDS AS ATTRIBUTION, QUALIFY SCOPE**. Mason's proposal is in fact broader than Josephus, and is contested. The **2007** essay was available with page markers and the author's uploaded journal text.
- **Primary author passage directly read:** Steve Mason, [“Jews, Judaeans, Judaizing, Judaism: Problems of Categorization in Ancient History,” *Journal for the Study of Judaism* **38.4–5** (2007): **457–512**](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233652312_Jews_Judaeans_Judaizing_Judaism_Problems_of_Categorization_in_Ancient_History), **pp. 457–458** (preface about editorial “Judean” choices in his Josephus series), **pp. 505–510** (Josephus and ethnic identity), and **pp. 511–512** (explicitly recommends “Judaean” as the most adequate English option for *Ioudaios / Iudaeus* across ancient sources). [DOI record](https://doi.org/10.1163/156851507X193108). This supports an account of **his view**, not consensus.
- **Critical independent counterarguments:** Adèle Reinhartz, [“The Vanishing Jews of Antiquity” (2014)](https://themarginaliareview.com/vanishing-jews-antiquity-adele-reinhartz/), directly engages Mason's 2007 essay and argues blanket substitution can erase continuity of Jews across eras without solving Johannine anti-Jewish reception. Cynthia Baker, [“A ‘Jew’ by Any Other Name?” *Journal of Ancient Judaism* **2.2** (2011): **153–180**, abstract](https://doi.org/10.30965/21967954-00202002), weighs Mason alongside other models and challenges their neat ethnic-versus-religious categories. Seth Schwartz, [University of Pennsylvania presentation abstract (2010)](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/psco/year48/1010.shtml), critiques the wider “Judaismlessness” argument.
- **Counterevidence/uncertainty:** “Judean”/“Judaean” is a translation strategy sensitive to text/context; applying a blanket policy across Josephus and all NT passages, or assuming the choice removes antisemitic uses, exceeds evidence. Don't collapse Mason's technical historical argument into a modern moral claim.
- **Safe proposed wording:** “Historian Steve Mason argues in a 2007 study that ‘Judaean’ better expresses the ethnic and political meaning of Greek *Ioudaios* in Josephus and other ancient writings; he uses ‘Judean’ in his Josephus translation project. His recommendation is disputed by scholars who argue that a blanket switch from ‘Jew’ can obscure historical Jewish continuity and reception of anti-Jewish texts.”
- **Confidence:** **High** for Mason's published view, **contested** as general translation recommendation. **Action:** eligible for **qualified attribution** promotion through synchronized public change.
