# Timeline App — Research Landscape (Deliverable A, C, D, E, F, G)

**Status:** Research documentation, synthesized from eight parallel live-search research passes. **This document asserts no historical facts of its own** — it maps what other researchers have already established (and disputed), so this project's research pipeline (SPEC.md §8) has real ground to verify against. Companion document `docs/timeline/research/DATASETS.md` carries the full per-source tables this synthesis draws on; every dataset/scholar named below is tabled there with citation and URL.

## A. Existing Research Landscape

The central question — *given a society's institutions, incentives, resources, and restrictions, which human capabilities increased, which stagnated, and how were benefits/costs distributed* — is not a new question. It is the organizing question of at least six overlapping, mature academic fields, each with its own instruments, journals, and decades of accumulated debate:

1. **Cliometrics / economic history** — long-run GDP, wages, inequality, living standards (Maddison Project, Clio-Infra, GPIH, WID). The methodological center of gravity for anything monetary.
2. **Comparative historical sociology / "cliodynamics"** — Seshat's attempt to quantify social complexity and state formation across all of deep history, sitting adjacent to but methodologically distinct from economic history (expert-coded qualitative judgment turned into structured variables, not administrative-record compilation).
3. **Comparative politics / political economy** — V-Dem, Polity5, Freedom House, BMR for regime type; Hanson & Sigman, O'Reilly-Murphy, Dincecco for state capacity. This field is where the "does democracy cause growth/health" literature (§E below) mostly lives.
4. **Bioarchaeology / anthropometric history** — the Global History of Health Project and the Steckel/Komlos anthropometric tradition, using skeletal and documentary-stature evidence to reconstruct biological living standards independent of monetary records — the only family of methods that reaches into genuinely pre-literate societies.
5. **History of science and technology** — a field explicitly organized around the empirical-capability-vs-mechanistic-explanation distinction this project's owner independently arrived at (§7.6 of DATASETS.md's cross-cultural findings); Isis Current Bibliography is its bibliographic backbone.
6. **Cross-cultural anthropology** — the Murdock tradition (Ethnographic Atlas → SCCS → D-PLACE) and, for religion specifically, the Database of Religious History — the primary machinery for genuinely global (not just literate-state) comparison, at the cost of being cross-sectional rather than historical.

**A structural fact that should shape the app's whole architecture**: these fields do not share a unit of analysis, a time grain, or an evidentiary standard. Economic history counts money; cliodynamics counts polities; bioarchaeology counts skeletons; anthropology counts societies at one ethnographic moment. **There is no field that already does what this app wants to do** — hold all of these frames simultaneously, on one timeline, with honest confidence labeling. That is a genuine gap this project would be filling, not duplicating. The corollary is that *building the app is mostly an integration and honesty-labeling problem, not a primary-research problem* — the raw material overwhelmingly already exists (see §F for the real exceptions).

## C. Measurement Map

For each capability/outcome category from the original research brief, this maps **what actually gets used to measure it, and how the evidentiary basis changes by era.** "—" means no dataset-grade source was found for that era; a named proxy with a caveat means it exists but should render in the app with an explicit confidence downgrade.

| Category | Prehistoric | Ancient/Bronze–Iron Age | Medieval/early modern | Industrial | Modern |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Health/survival, life expectancy, infant mortality** | Skeletal age-at-death (paleodemography, osteological-paradox-limited) | Same, plus early administrative census fragments (rare) | Parish-register-based demography (Princeton EFP-adjacent methods) | Human Mortality Database begins for high-data-quality states | HMD, full vital statistics |
| **Nutrition, stature, disease burden** | Global History of Health Project skeletal codebook; stable isotopes for diet | Same methods, richer textual corroboration (ration records, e.g. Mesopotamian administrative tablets) | Anthropometric-history conscript/enlistee records begin (with known selection bias) | Denser conscript/institutional height records | Direct anthropometric surveys |
| **Agricultural productivity, food security** | Archaeobotanical/zooarchaeological remains, settlement-pattern inference | Administrative ration/yield records (Mesopotamia, Egypt) — genuinely primary but local, not aggregable to "GDP" | Land/tithe records | National agricultural statistics begin | FAO-grade data |
| **Wages, living standards** | — | — (grain-ration equivalents exist but aren't "wages" in a market sense) | GPIH/Allen-Unger welfare ratios (contested — see §E) | Denser urban wage series | National statistics |
| **Wealth/income inequality, land concentration** | House-size Gini (Kohler/Smith — the *only* method reaching this far back) | Milanovic/Lindert/Williamson "social tables" (sparse point estimates) | Tax-record Ginis (Alfani), land-record Ginis (Deininger-Squire-adjacent) | Probate/estate records | WID tax-based DINA series |
| **Slavery/forced labor** | — | Legal-code and administrative evidence, case-by-case | Slave Voyages (Atlantic only — trans-Saharan/Indian Ocean/Red Sea trades have no comparable dataset) | Same, plus abolition-era administrative records | UN/ILO modern forced-labor estimates |
| **Literacy, education** | — | — | Clio-Infra signature-rate and age-heaping-numeracy proxies begin to have force | Benavot-Riddle/Lindert/Barro-Lee enrollment series | UNESCO literacy statistics |
| **Scientific knowledge/output, technology, engineering** | Material-culture/technological-complexity inference only | Direct textual evidence in literate societies (see DATASETS.md §7's non-European capability table) | Manuscript-production proxy (Buringh & van Zanden — Europe-only) | Patent counts (contested proxy — see DATASETS.md §7) | Publication/patent counts, with the same institutional-adoption-vs-innovation caveat |
| **State capacity, taxation, bureaucracy** | — | Administrative-tablet density as an informal proxy; no structured dataset | Dincecco (Western Europe, 1650+) is the only genuinely early-modern fiscal-capacity source | O'Reilly-Murphy composite begins (1789) | Hanson-Sigman (1960+), V-Dem sub-indices |
| **Political participation, democracy/autocracy** | — | — | — | BMR/Polity5 begin (1800) | V-Dem, Freedom House |
| **Censorship, freedom of inquiry** | — | — | — (case-study literature only — Darnton on Old Regime France) | — | V-Dem expression sub-index as the only proxy with real depth; RSF/Van Belle-Whitten from ~2002 only — **this is a confirmed dataset gap, not a search failure** (DATASETS.md §6) |
| **Religious freedom, religious institutional power** | — | — | DRH system-level coding, where entries exist | RAS begins (1990) | Pew GRI/SHI (2007+), RAS, ARDA |
| **Gender inequality dimensions (six, per SPEC §7.7)** | House-size/grave-goods inference only, heavily contested | EA/D-PLACE structural snapshot *if* the society was later ethnographically documented (rarely applicable this far back) — mostly legal-code/administrative-record case study | Same legal/administrative case-study method, denser record survival | SIGI/GID-DB begin (present-day only, ~2009+) | SIGI, WVS, national statistics |
| **Warfare, interpersonal violence** | Skeletal trauma (see DATASETS.md §2 proxy table) | Same, plus textual campaign records | Eisner's court-record homicide reconstruction (Western Europe only — confirmed non-European gap) | COW begins (1816) | UCDP/PRIO, COW, Eisner-tradition national homicide statistics |
| **Imprisonment/state coercion** | — | — | — | — (no historical dataset exists at all — confirmed gap) | World Prison Brief (current cross-section only) |
| **Environmental degradation, energy use, resource consumption** | Pollen/sediment/ice-core proxies | Same, plus early metallurgical/deforestation archaeological evidence | Same | HYDE land-use reconstruction becomes denser | Direct environmental statistics |

**The pattern worth naming explicitly, because it should shape the app's confidence-rendering logic**: the transition from "no structured source" to "a contested proxy" to "a real dataset" does not happen at the same calendar date for different categories. Wealth inequality gets a (crude) deep-time proxy via house size that literacy, censorship, and imprisonment never get at all. **The app's timeline should visually communicate this unevenness rather than implying uniform data quality across categories at a given date** — a Z2-zoom view of 1000 BCE should look very different in "how much do we actually know" depending on which capability panel is open.

## D. Systems/Institutions Map

What exists for measuring the *institutional conditions* the app wants to compare outcomes against (full detail in DATASETS.md §1, §5, §6):

- **Regime type / democracy-autocracy**: V-Dem (deepest, most granular, uncertainty-quantified, 1789+), Polity5 (frozen at 2018, methodologically critiqued by Munck & Verkuilen but still the most-replicated), BMR (simplest, most transparent, best robustness-check), Freedom House (longest single continuous methodology, 1972+). **Recommendation: treat V-Dem as primary, the other three as robustness/cross-validation, not independent primary sources** — they substantially measure the same underlying construct with different aggregation choices.
- **State capacity**: genuinely three different maturity tiers — Dincecco (early-modern, Western-Europe-only, real archival primary data), O'Reilly-Murphy (1789+, derivative of V-Dem), Hanson-Sigman (1960+ only, the political-science field standard but useless for anything actually historical). **There is no global, deep-time, primary state-capacity source** — this is a real gap the app must represent honestly, not paper over with V-Dem's rule-of-law sub-index dressed up as "capacity."
- **Religious institutions**: DRH (system-level, historical, entry-driven coverage) for what a tradition's doctrine/institutions *were*; RAS/Pew GRI-SHI/ARDA (1990s+ only) for state-religion *policy*; World Religion Database (commercial) for *demography*. These three answer three different questions and should not be treated as substitutes for each other.
- **Legal/constitutional systems**: Comparative Constitutions Project (primary-text-coded, 1789+, but codes *what constitutions say*, not enforcement — must be paired with an outcome measure, never used alone as a governance indicator).
- **Kinship/family/gender structural systems**: Ethnographic Atlas/D-PLACE/SCCS — the only genuinely global, structured comparative source, at the cost of being one cross-sectional snapshot per society rather than a time series (full detail and the "don't treat this as diachronic" warning in DATASETS.md §4).
- **Conflict/violence institutions**: UCDP (1946+/1989+ for GED), COW (1816+, incompatible threshold with UCDP), PRIO (frozen 2008). None reach pre-modern institutions of organized violence at all except via Seshat's coded warfare variables, which inherit Seshat's own "data pasting/filling" critique.

## E. Literature Map — Major Scholarly Debates on Systems ↔ Outcomes

The brief explicitly asked not to search only for confirming evidence. What follows is deliberately structured around **live, named disputes** — every pairing below has a real counter-finding or credible methodological challenge, not just a mainstream position. Full citations in DATASETS.md is not where these live — they're compiled here since they're literature, not datasets; full citations are inline.

| Pairing | Mainstream finding | Real counter-evidence / null finding | Causal identification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Democracy ↔ health/education** | ~11 years higher life expectancy, 62.5% lower infant mortality in electoral democracies (Bollyky et al., *Lancet* 2019) | Association attenuates sharply once GDP per capita is controlled; a 2025 *Oxford Economic Papers* review states there is "a shortage of clear and convincing evidence for a causal relationship" | Mostly observational/fixed-effects; some natural-experiment leverage (WHO eradication campaigns as an exogenous baseline; Brazil's electronic-voting rollout) |
| **Democracy ↔ growth/innovation** | Acemoglu, Naidu, Restrepo & Robinson, *JPE* 2019 (NBER w20004): democratization raises GDP per capita ~20% over 25 years | Doucouliagos & Ulubasoglu meta-analysis of 483 estimates/84 studies (*AJPS* 2008): **no robust direct effect** — any growth benefit is indirect via human capital/inflation/stability. A CEPR replication shows ANRR's headline estimate is sensitive to a small number of non-randomly-dropped observations, roughly halving under alternative specs | One of the better-identified corners of the field (regional-democratization-wave instruments, dynamic panels) — but the meta-analytic picture shows real heterogeneity underneath |
| **Autocracy ↔ state capacity** | No general "autocratic advantage" is empirically supported — capacity clusters with cohesive political institutions (Besley & Persson), not regime type | Dan Slater, *Ordering Power* (2010): Southeast Asian state strength traces to specific elite "protection pacts" under threat conditions, not authoritarianism per se. Andersson & Teorell (2025): state capacity extends an individual autocrat's *tenure* but makes the *regime* more fragile — stronger autocracies more likely to democratize | Almost entirely comparative-historical; "autocracy" is endogenous to the same historical processes (war, elite coalitions, colonial legacy) that produce state capacity |
| **Capitalism/markets ↔ productivity/living standards** | Bundle-of-factors consensus: market integration, property-rights institutions, Allen's "high-wage economy" labor-saving-innovation thesis, Pomeranz's coal-geography-plus-New-World-windfall account | East Asian "developmental state" literature (Chalmers Johnson 1982, Alice Amsden 1989, Robert Wade 1990) documents rapid postwar productivity growth achieved through heavy state industrial policy — directly contra a laissez-faire "markets alone" story | Mostly historical-comparative/growth-accounting — inherently hard to identify causally from single historical episodes |
| **Capitalism ↔ inequality** | Piketty's r>g thesis: structural wealth concentration absent countervailing shocks/taxation | Matthew Rognlie (Brookings Papers 2015): once depreciation is properly netted, the capital-labor substitution elasticity is below 1, reversing Piketty's mechanics. **Auten & Splinter, *JPE* 2023: US top-1% pre-tax share rose only 9%→14% since 1960 (not 9%→20%), with virtually no rise after taxes/transfers** — a direct empirical rebuttal built on the same underlying tax data, not a different dataset | Almost entirely a measurement/data-construction dispute, not a causally-identified one — the fight is over how to build the series, not over a design |
| **State socialism ↔ health/education** | Navarro's matched comparisons (1970s-80s): more favorable outcomes in 30 of 36 socialist-vs-capitalist country pairs at similar income | Cuba-specific counterfactual literature stresses pre-revolutionary Cuba already had life expectancy 8 years above the regional average; a naive comparison overstates socialism's causal share. Eastern European life expectancy *diverged negatively* from Western Europe from the late 1960s, only narrowing post-2000 — the opposite pattern in that region | Almost entirely matched-comparison, not causally identified; counterfactual construction is explicitly acknowledged as the core unsolved problem by researchers working on this |
| **State socialism ↔ innovation/productivity** | Soviet TFP rose to ~2.8%/year in the 1950s "golden period," then fell monotonically, turning negative in the 1980s (Kukić 2024) — consensus attributes this to allocative inefficiency and the absence of price-based rational calculation | Post-Soviet transition research notes "beneficial legacy effects... disappeared" only by 2008 — implying real transitional benefits existed (military/aerospace R&D, basic science education) that simply didn't commercialize, complicating a flat "planning kills innovation" reading | Historical growth-accounting/case-study; split-country natural experiments (East/West Germany, the two Koreas) are informally cited but not rigorously econometrically treated in what this research pass surfaced |
| **Empire/colonialism ↔ infrastructure & development** | Persistence-of-underdevelopment side: Nunn (*QJE* 2008) on the slave trade; **Dell (*Econometrica* 2010) — regression discontinuity at Peru's historical mita boundary, one of the field's cleanest causal designs**; AJR (*AER* 2001) instrument institutions with settler mortality | Infrastructure-persistence side: colonial railway studies (India, Ghana, Kenya) find durable *positive* local effects via urban path-dependence — directly complicating a uniform "extractive institutions = bad outcomes" story. **David Albouy (*AER* 2012): 36 of 64 countries in AJR's sample were assigned mortality data from *other* countries via sourcing errors; correcting this makes the IV estimates lose robustness** — AJR's reply drops ~60% of the original sample to defend the result, a genuinely unresolved standoff | The best-identified pairing in this whole map (RD, IV, panel-with-placebo designs on both sides) — and still contested at the level of data integrity, not just interpretation |
| **Religiosity/institutions ↔ literacy/economic outcomes** | Becker & Woessmann (*QJE* 2009): Prussian county data, distance-to-Wittenberg instrument — Protestantism raised prosperity via literacy/human capital, *not* a Weberian work ethic | **Davide Cantoni (*JEEA* 2015): 272-city panel, 1300–1900 — finds NO effect of Protestantism on city population growth**, robust to IV, with evidence of *negative selection* (economically peripheral cities more likely to adopt the Reformation) | Both sides use real IV designs and reach opposite conclusions depending on outcome measure and sample scope — a genuinely live, unresolved identification dispute between two well-executed studies, not one side lacking rigor |
| **Religious monopoly/establishment ↔ freedom of inquiry/scientific output** | Eric Chaney (Harvard working paper): trend-break analysis finds Islamic scientific output declined as religious elites' *political power* (not religiosity per se) increased, shifting talent toward madrasa/religious knowledge production | A 27-country firm-level panel found a *positive* religiosity-innovation (patent) association in Judeo-Christian-majority countries, while other cross-country/within-US work finds a *robust negative* relationship — genuinely unconverged literature, and little of it isolates "monopoly/establishment" as the actual treatment variable rather than religiosity broadly | Weak overall — Chaney's design is structural/historical time-series, not RD/IV/DID; no natural experiment isolating monopoly specifically was found |
| **Warfare ↔ state capacity ("war made the state")** | Tilly's thesis holds within its original early-modern-Europe scope — recent work explicitly reconfirms it there | **Jeffrey Herbst, *States and Power in Africa* (2000): the ABSENCE of interstate war (low population density, no existential territorial threats) is precisely what prevented African states from consolidating — the opposite mechanism.** Miguel Centeno, *Blood and Debt* (2002): Latin American wars were "limited wars" financed by foreign debt, producing "limited states," not Tilly-style consolidation | Qualitative-comparative case-study method throughout — no RD/IV/DID design found for this pairing despite its prominence; consensus among Tilly's own critics is the mechanism is real but historically contingent on specifically European conditions |
| **Trade/urbanization ↔ knowledge diffusion, disease burden** | Network-centrality of medieval settlements predicted Black Death severity better than population size alone (*Scientific Reports* 2017); modern urbanization-innovation panels (China-focused) find positive network-embeddedness effects | Within the same patent-transfer literature: hub cities benefit from inflows while *outflow* crowds out innovation in less-developed cities — diffusion is not uniformly positive-sum | Weakest identification of any pairing surveyed — mostly structural/simulation-based (Black Death) or China-specific quasi-natural-experiments not yet replicated cross-nationally |

**Religion-science relationship literature** (a related but distinct debate, full detail in DATASETS.md's history-of-science section): the 19th-century "conflict thesis" (Draper/White) is **settled historiographical consensus as discredited** — Ronald Numbers's *Galileo Goes to Jail* (Harvard UP, 2009) and John Hedley Brooke's *Science and Religion* (Cambridge UP, 1991) established the field's replacement "complexity thesis." What remains genuinely unsettled: whether religious/supernatural explanations generally *retreat* as natural knowledge advances, or get *reinterpreted/accommodated* — both dynamics are independently well-documented (the lightning-rod controversy for retreat; the long patristic-to-modern accommodation tradition for reinterpretation) with no single governing model in the literature. **This directly bears on the owner's core hypothesis (SPEC.md §7.6/HANDOFF.md §58): the honest answer is "genuinely case-dependent, not a settled correlation in either direction."**

## F. Data Gaps — What Cannot Currently Be Measured Reliably

Stated plainly, per this project's own rule that "unknown" is a legitimate result:

1. **Historical censorship/freedom-of-inquiry**: no dedicated dataset exists at any time depth. V-Dem's expression sub-index is the only proxy with real historical reach, and it is not a censorship-dedicated instrument.
2. **Historical imprisonment/state coercion**: no cross-national historical dataset exists at all, at any time depth. Only a contemporary cross-section (World Prison Brief) plus scattered single-country (mostly US) administrative series.
3. **Apostasy/heresy law across history**: no structured dataset; comparative legal-history scholarship only.
4. **Women's historical property/inheritance rights, as a time series**: no D-PLACE-equivalent exists. EA/D-PLACE inheritance variables are single-snapshot, not diachronic; everything genuinely historical is case-study legal history.
5. **Religious institutional control of education, comparatively**: no cross-tradition dataset; strong single-tradition case material (Nizamiyya madrasas, Nalanda, European monastic schools) never assembled comparatively.
6. **Individual-level religiosity before the survey era (pre-1981)**: no equivalent to WVS exists; proxies (church-attendance records, will/bequest patterns) are geographically and confessionally narrow and explicitly flagged as weak by the scholars who use them.
7. **Non-European long-run interpersonal-violence/homicide history**: confirmed, named gap — Eisner's tradition is overwhelmingly Western/Northern European; UNODC's global homicide data only reaches back to 2006.
8. **Pre-modern GDP and inequality as point-precise figures**: not a gap in existence (Maddison, Milanovic et al. do produce numbers) but a **precision gap** — every source surveyed independently converges on treating pre-1820 GDP and pre-industrial Gini figures as wide, usually unquantified plausible ranges, not measurements with error bars. Gregory Clark's critique of Maddison and the Stephenson-vs-Allen wage dispute both stem from this same underlying problem.
9. **Historical state fiscal/administrative capacity outside Western Europe, pre-1789**: Dincecco's is the only genuinely archival early-modern source, and it covers 11 Western European states only.
10. **A single non-Atlantic slave-trade dataset**: Slave Voyages is rigorous but Atlantic-only; the trans-Saharan, Red Sea, and Indian Ocean slave trades have no comparable quantitative source.

**None of these ten should be silently interpolated or estimated by the app.** Per SPEC.md §6.4, every one becomes an explicit `evidence_absence_note`, not a filled cell.

## G. Recommended MVP Dataset

Given everything above, the smallest defensible collection that lets the app prototype its core interaction (compare institutions and outcomes across time without pretending to know more than historians do) without overclaiming:

**Tier 1 — ingest directly, high confidence, genuinely long-run:**
- V-Dem (regime type/institutions, 1789+, with its own uncertainty intervals — ingest those intervals, don't collapse to a point estimate)
- Maddison Project (GDP/capita, with an explicit precision downgrade applied automatically before 1820, per Gap #8 above)
- Clio-Infra (literacy, stature, wages — flagged per-indicator since methodology varies by contributing scholar)
- UCDP (1946+) + COW (1816+), kept as two *separate*, non-concatenated series per their own stated incompatibility
- WID (inequality, with the Auten-Splinter dispute rendered as a live disagreement, not resolved silently in Piketty-Saez's favor)

**Tier 2 — ingest as deep-time proxies with mandatory confidence downgrades and method labels:**
- Kohler/Smith house-size Gini (the only pre-1000-BCE inequality signal available at all)
- Global History of Health Project stature/pathology data (if the live-access question from DATASETS.md §2 resolves favorably — verify before building an ingestion pipeline around it)
- Eisner's homicide reconstruction (Western Europe only — the app must not imply global coverage by omission)
- Ethnographic Atlas/D-PLACE (kinship/gender structural variables, explicitly non-diachronic)

**Tier 3 — do not ingest as data; use as citable case-study/qualitative content instead:**
- Everything flagged as a confirmed gap in §F above (censorship, imprisonment, apostasy law, women's property rights as a time series, religious-education institutions, pre-1981 religiosity) — these become sourced prose entries in the claim store (per SPEC.md's `Claim` schema), not chart data, until/unless a real dataset is found or built.

**Explicitly deferred, not because it's unimportant but because it needs its own research pass before ingestion**: the systems↔outcomes causal literature in §E above should NOT be compressed into "X causes Y" claims in the MVP. Each pairing should render as a `Hypothesis` object (SPEC.md §7.6) with the mainstream finding and the named counter-evidence both linked, exactly as tabled in §E — this is the literature's own honest state, and the app's job is to make that visible, not to resolve it prematurely.
