# Comparative Constitutional and Legal-Systems Module

**Status:** Research queue and country-profile template.

## Country-profile fields

- Constitutional position: state religion, religious source of law, secularism/separation, or neutral constitutional framework.
- Religion-linked legal fields: family, inheritance, criminal law, education, dress, worship, public office, funding, registration, and courts.
- Forum: civil court, religious court, mixed court, administrative body, or community tribunal.
- Scope: persons, community, territory, subject matter, and exceptions.
- Primary authority: official constitution, statute, code, court ruling, or gazette.
- Classification, status, explanatory authority, confidence, last-verified date, and cautions.

## Initial jurisdiction queue

### Explicit constitutional religious basis

- **Saudi Arabia:** Basic Law of Governance (1992), Article 1 identifies Islam as the state religion and the Qur’an and Sunnah as the constitution.
- **Iran:** Constitution, Article 4 requires civil, penal, financial, economic, administrative, cultural, military, political, and other laws and regulations to be based on Islamic criteria.
- **Pakistan:** Constitutional Islamic framework and Penal Code §§ 295–298 should be separated into constitutional and section-by-section criminal-law records.

### Personal-law and religious-court systems

- **India:** Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act 1937; Hindu Marriage Act 1955; Special Marriage Act; relevant court decisions; scope must be statute- and community-specific.
- **Israel:** Rabbinical Courts Jurisdiction (Marriage and Divorce) Law 1953; record Jewish marriage/divorce jurisdiction separately from other family-law forums and foreign-marriage recognition.
- **Malaysia, Indonesia, Nigeria, Kenya:** Research civil/religious court divisions by territory, religious community, and legal subject.

### Secularism and state-religion comparison

- **France:** Law of 9 December 1905, especially Articles 1–2; Law No. 2004-228 on conspicuous religious signs in public schools.
- **United Kingdom:** Established Church of England and human-rights/equality framework.
- **Turkey, Mexico, Canada, Germany, Brazil, South Africa, Japan, Australia:** create discrete constitutional, statutory, funding, education, equality, and accommodation records.

## Method warning

A constitutional religious clause does not by itself establish the content or enforcement of every law in a country. Record implementation, judicial review, territorial exceptions, and minority-rights claims separately.
