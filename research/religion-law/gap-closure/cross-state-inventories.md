# Cross-State Religion & Law Inventories

**Status:** Anchor-state pass complete (2026-10-20). All four tables are populated for the eight expanded anchor states: AR, CT, LA, MA, MD, ME, MT and TX. The other 42 states are not started.
**Scope:** Structured collection layer for state-profile gaps
**Promotion rule:** No row becomes a state-profile conclusion until the current primary legal authority and legal status are verified.

These inventories are factual discovery aids. A statute's existence does not establish religious causation, and a religiously specific exemption or accommodation is normally classified by its legal function rather than by inferred motive.

**Verification method for this pass:**
- Each row was read from the current official code page, or from Justia's current-year code where the official site does not render statute text to automated readers (marked "Justia").
- A verbatim operative passage from each source was machine-matched against the fetched text before the row was written.
- One machine extraction error was caught and corrected: the summarizer reported a clergy confidential-communication carve-out in Connecticut's reporting law, which does not appear in the statute.
- **"Key cases" is not compiled in this pass.** Cells reading "Not compiled" are a gap, not a finding that no cases exist.

## Cross-state observations (descriptive, not causal)

- **Tax:** All eight states exempt houses of worship. The variation is in add-ons: parsonage caps (ME $20,000), acreage caps (MT), and organizational tests (TX §11.20(c); LA federal-exempt status).
- **Healthcare conscience:**
  - Four of eight tie the right to stated grounds: AR (religious, moral or ethical), LA (religious belief or moral conviction), MA (written moral or religious grounds) and MT (religious beliefs or moral convictions).
  - Connecticut's clause covers "judgment, philosophical, moral or religious beliefs."
  - Three (MD, ME, TX) protect refusal without requiring any stated ground.
  - Arkansas has the only all-services conscience statute; Louisiana's is limited to six listed services.
- **Clergy privilege and reporting:**
  - All eight states have a clergy privilege, and all eight make clergy mandated reporters.
  - Six (AR, LA, MA, MD, ME, MT) exempt confidential or penitential communications from the reporting duty.
  - Connecticut's reporting statute has no clergy-specific carve-out.
  - Texas states the duty applies "without exception" to clergy.
- **Employment:** Every state exempts religious employers in some form. The forms differ: a whole-category exclusion (AR, MT), co-religionist preference with a religious-principles test (MA, TX), ground-specific exemptions (CT, MD, ME), or an education-only preference (LA).

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. Current state constitution/statutory code or official court rule.
2. Session law and official legislative history where historical development matters.
3. Controlling state/federal cases.
4. Official agency guidance.
5. Reputable secondary legal scholarship for context and source discovery.

Commercial summaries may identify leads but do not verify a row.
