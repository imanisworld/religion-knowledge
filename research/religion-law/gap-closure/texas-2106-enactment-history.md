**Critical archival caveat (LRL's own note):** The *introduced version* and *Senate Committee Report version* of SB 34 are both missing from the bill file. Surviving primary material is therefore limited to the House side — HB 514 was considered on several dates in subcommittee and full committee hearings before SB 34 reached the House, meaning the substantive record of floor and committee debate is concentrated in the House bill file, not the Senate bill file.

### Origin of the revision project

The 1973 code concluded an **eight-year professional legal reform process** begun in 1965. That timeline alone does not establish the motives for each provision:

- **1965:** State Bar of Texas forms the Committee on Revision of the Penal Code. Chair: Page Keeton, Dean, University of Texas School of Law.
- **1966–1970:** Staff of the Texas Legislative Council and Bar Committee draft chapters, drawing heavily on the **Model Penal Code** (American Law Institute, 1962) and surveying all fifty state codes.
- **1971:** HB 419 (62nd Leg.) introduced but fails — irreconcilable differences on specific sections; tabled. HCR 184 creates an interim study committee.
- **1971–1973:** Bar Committee, Texas District and County Attorneys Association (TDCAA), and Texas Criminal Defense Lawyers Association (TCDLA) reach a tripartite consensus on disputed language (documented on the LRL Penal Code legislative trail, https://lrl.texas.gov/collections/PenalCodeTrail.cfm; the LRL timeline page does not name TDCAA or TCDLA).
- **1973:** SB 34/HB 514 enacted.

The **documented codewide drafting and negotiation actors** were professional legal associations (State Bar, TDCAA, TCDLA). The LRL trail does not establish that these were the only actors influencing **§21.06**, or that religious organizations were absent from every hearing.

## Why §21.06 is same-sex specific: the Model Penal Code departure

This is the structural key. The American Law Institute's **Model Penal Code (1962)** had already voted in 1955 to **decriminalize consensual adult sodomy entirely** — it contained no sodomy offense whatsoever. Texas's revision team used the MPC as its foundation but expressly departed from it on specific retained provisions.

§21.06 represents a **partial MPC departure**: Texas chose to:
1. Follow the MPC in decriminalizing opposite-sex consensual adult sodomy (eliminating the 1943 statute's gender-neutral coverage of anal and oral sex between adults).
2. **Depart from the MPC** by retaining a criminal offense targeting same-sex conduct only.

The AMA Journal of Ethics confirms this pattern nationally: "Kansas, Texas, Montana, Kentucky, Missouri, Nevada, and Tennessee decriminalized opposite-sex consensual sodomy, leaving consensual same-sex sodomy as a misdemeanor crime." This was a documented national legislative pattern — states in the 1960s–70s reform wave that adopted a modified MPC often retained same-sex-specific provisions as a political compromise to secure passage of the broader reform package.

The UTEP thesis (Ross 2016) establishes the textual lineage directly: the 1973 §21.06 definition ("any contact between any part of the genitals of one person and the mouth or anus of another person [of the same sex]") is rooted in a **portion** of the 1943 Texas sodomy statute, which was itself gender-neutral. The 1973 act **narrowed** the 1943 provision from a general sodomy offense to a same-sex-specific one — a structural choice made within the professional revision process, not through introduction of new religious pressure.

## What the evidence establishes about causation

### Direct documentary evidence of religious advocacy into the 1973 proceedings

**None identified.** The LRL records describe the driving negotiation as among the State Bar, TDCAA, and TCDLA. No witness lists, committee testimony records, or floor journal entries attributing §21.06's same-sex specificity to religious-group advocacy have been located in accessible sources. The Senate committee report version of SB 34 is missing from the bill file, which limits what can be confirmed from that side.

### What is well-documented

1. **Structural religious inheritance (deep background):** The word "sodomy" itself has religious roots (medieval Christian theology, Sodom narrative). The Ross (2016) thesis traces this explicitly: "Sodomy is a religiously rooted term engendered by a medieval interpretation of the biblical story about the sin and destruction of the city of Sodom." This inheritance shaped the centuries-long legal tradition that Texas's 1860, 1943, and 1973 statutes participated in.

2. **Broader national religious-opposition pattern (context, not proof):** The AMA Journal of Ethics notes that "[s]tates that adopted the revised MPC saw tremendous protests from religious groups and right-wing political interests" and that "[o]ther states … balked at such proposed reforms, arguing that changes to sodomy laws promoted homosexuality and unnatural conduct." This establishes that religious pressure existed in the national environment — but it does not document its operation specifically in the 63rd Texas Legislature's deliberations.

3. **Moral-public-policy framing (secular form):** The *Lawrence* majority (Kennedy, J.) characterized laws aimed at same-sex couples as "motivated by animus towards homosexuals that arose from religious and moral condemnation," but this is a judicial retrospective characterization in 2003, not a 1973 enactment record. *Bowers v. Hardwick* (1986) had earlier held that moral disapproval alone was a sufficient rational basis — neither decision locates a specifically religious cause for the Texas law's enactment.

4. **Professional-legal consensus mechanism — codewide evidence only:** The LRL documents agreement among the Bar/TDCAA/TCDLA groups on a revised **Penal Code**. It does **not** provide a verified passage about why those negotiators or legislators retained **§21.06's same-sex-only language**. A political compromise is plausible, but neither its precise participants nor the absence of religious advocacy on this specific section has been established.

**No §21.06-specific religious-causation claim is established by the located enactment sources.** The statute's same-sex-only wording is **consistent with**, but cannot yet be attributed specifically to, compromise in the professional legal codification process. That codewide process and the broader religious and moral history of sodomy restrictions are documented. The surviving files do not settle which considerations drove the 1973 vote on §21.06. Neither a religious-lobby cause nor its absence may be asserted.
