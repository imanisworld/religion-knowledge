# Texas Penal Code §21.06 — 1973 Enactment History

**Status:** 🔴 Open  
**Target profile:** `../us-states/texas.md`  
**Question:** What documentary evidence explains the 1973 Texas Legislature's decision to enact a same-sex-specific offense in Penal Code §21.06?

## Existing verified baseline

The Texas profile already verifies the statute's text/status, its invalidation by *Lawrence v. Texas*, and the broader historical distinction between older sodomy-law traditions and the specific 1973 Texas formulation. Do not change those findings without contrary evidence.

## Research tasks

1. Identify the 63rd Legislature bill(s), revision-commission materials, and codification trail that produced §21.06.
2. Locate committee referrals, bill analyses, hearing materials, witness lists, and available testimony.
3. Review House and Senate journals for amendments, floor discussion, conference action, and recorded explanations.
4. Search contemporaneous Texas reporting and archival commentary for stated rationales and organized advocacy.
5. Separate:
   - direct religious advocacy or explicitly religious legislative reasoning;
   - nonreligious morality/public-policy reasoning;
   - inherited legal language or revision-commission drafting;
   - later retrospective characterizations.
6. Conclude one of:
   - **documented religious causal role**;
   - **mixed documented causes**;
   - **primarily secular documented purpose**;
   - **insufficient enactment-specific evidence / unresolved**.

## Causation gate

A stronger religion-causation claim requires enactment-specific evidence of chronology, a plausible transmission/advocacy channel, and meaningful correspondence. Broader American sodomy-law history is context, not proof of the 1973 Texas Legislature's motive.
