# Montana 1972 No-Aid Clause — Convention History

**Status:** Closed (2026-10-20). Verdict: **Mixed. Secular and institutional reasons led, with active religious politics on both sides. The 1972 record does not show anti-Catholic animus driving the readoption, but it also did not remove the inherited "sectarian" wording.**
**Target profile:** `../us-states/montana.md`
**Provision:** Montana Constitution art. X, §6 (Committee of the Whole debate, March 11, 1972)

## What happened

1. **Delegate proposals:** No. 41 (Leuthold) retained the 1889 text verbatim. No. 164 (Harbaugh) retained it but added that the section "shall not apply to federal funds administered by the state for the purposes of nonpublic education" ([UMT](https://www.umt.edu/montana-constitution/articles/article-x/x-6.php)).
2. **Committee split:** The Education and Public Lands Committee divided.
   - **Majority (Burkhardt):** keep the 1889 text, including "directly or indirectly."
   - **Minority (Harbaugh, with Harrington and others):** delete "or indirectly" and add a federal-funds exception ([UMT](https://www.umt.edu/montana-constitution/articles/article-x/x-6.php)).
3. **Floor substitute (Loendorf):** keep the majority's wording, including "indirectly," but add the federal-funds sentence. It carried **53 Aye, 40 No** (Tr. 2024–2025).
4. **Campbell amendment:** It would have replaced the final sentence with a broader "nonstate money" clause. It failed **13 Aye, 79 No**. Section 6 as amended was then adopted by voice vote.
5. **Net textual change from 1889:** The prohibition, including "sectarian," "sect," and "indirect," was carried forward. A new subsection (2) exempts "funds from federal sources provided to the state for the express purpose of distribution to non-public education."

## Analysis against the causation gate

- **Continuity with 1889:** Strong. The majority deliberately kept the 1889 words, and the adopted text kept them too, including "sect" and "sectarian."
- **Public-school support and church-state separation:** These were the majority's stated, contemporaneous reasons, and Protestant and unaffiliated delegates said so on the record.
- **Ratification politics:** The majority report itself named fear that the church-state issue "might endanger passage of the entire Constitution." That made retention a defensive, risk-avoidance choice.
- **Anti-Catholic motive in 1972:** No delegate in the matched pages defended the clause on anti-Catholic grounds. The anti-Catholic history was raised by Catholic delegates (Schiltz) as a reason to repeal, and was rebutted as a mischaracterization of separationism (Harper, Kelleher).
- **Religious alignment:** It did not track denomination. Catholic delegates argued on several sides (Schiltz and Driscoll for change; Kelleher for flexibility while rejecting the bigotry label). Self-identified Protestants backed the minority or the compromise (Brown, Blaylock, Choate) as well as the majority (Harper, McNeil). The Montana Catholic Conference, as reported by Campbell, sought only federal pass-through funding. The final compromise gave that federal-funds exception and nothing more.
- **What the record does not show:** It does not show the delegates "confronted" and rewrote the inherited sectarian terminology. Justice Alito's concurrence stresses this point; the dissents stress the religiously diverse support. Both are retrospective readings of the same transcript.

## Verdict

**Mixed causation for the 1972 readoption.** The documented drivers were:

- public-school protection;
- separationist principle, voiced in religious as well as secular terms;
- continuity;
- fear that the issue would sink ratification.

A narrowly negotiated federal-funds exception answered the parochial-school constituency. The clause's 1889 Blaine-era origin is a separate layer: it remains **B (historical religious origin)** for the inherited wording. It is not evidence that the 1972 delegates shared the 1889 motive.

**Confidence:** Medium-high for the account of the debate (primary transcript); medium for relative causal weight, because committee-hearing testimony and Vol. II report text were not read directly.
