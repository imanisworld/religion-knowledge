# Montana 1972 No-Aid Clause — Convention History

**Status:** 🔴 Open  
**Target profile:** `../us-states/montana.md`  
**Provision:** Montana Constitution art. X, §6

## Existing verified baseline

The Montana profile already distinguishes the 1889 no-aid provision's Blaine-era lineage from the 1972 Constitution's later readoption. *Espinoza v. Montana Department of Revenue* is controlling for the federal Free Exercise constraint discussed in the profile, but later judicial characterizations do not substitute for 1972 convention evidence about delegate motives.

## Research tasks

1. Locate the 1971–72 Constitutional Convention transcripts, committee reports, proposal text, delegate files, and voting history for what became Article X, §6.
2. Identify statements addressing:
   - continuity with the 1889 provision;
   - public-school funding;
   - church/state separation;
   - anti-Catholic or anti-sectarian concerns;
   - fiscal/institutional concerns independent of religious hostility;
   - proposed alternatives or amendments.
3. Attribute each motive to named delegates or official committee material.
4. Compare the final language with the 1889 text and document substantive changes.
5. Use *Espinoza* opinions only as retrospective legal framing, clearly separated from contemporaneous 1972 evidence.

## Causation gate

Do not transfer motives from the nineteenth-century Blaine movement directly onto the 1972 delegates. The 1972 readoption needs its own evidence.
