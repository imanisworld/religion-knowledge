**Status:** 🔴 Open

## Existing verified baseline

The Maine profile already verifies the current Sunday motor-vehicle sales restriction and the explicitly religious/“Lord's Day” architecture of the broader statutory chapter. It deliberately does **not** claim that Puritanism or Sabbatarianism directly caused the motor-vehicle-specific rule without provision-specific enactment evidence.

## Research tasks

1. Trace §3203 to its original enactment and every material amendment.
2. Identify the session law, bill/LD number, committee, sponsor, and legislative history for the motor-vehicle-specific restriction.
3. Determine whether the provision was:
   - carved out of a broader Sunday-closing regime;
   - requested by dealers or industry groups;
   - framed as labor/rest-day policy;
   - defended in Sabbatarian/religious terms;
   - supported by multiple motives.
4. Keep the provision-specific history separate from the older general Lord's Day statutes.
5. Record later amendments that materially changed purpose, scope, or exceptions.

## Causation gate

The placement of §3203 in an explicitly religious/holy-day statutory lineage is relevant historical evidence, but it is not by itself enough to attribute the motor-vehicle-specific enactment to a religious cause.
