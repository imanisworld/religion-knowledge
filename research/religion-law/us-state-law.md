# U.S. State-Law Module

**Status:** Research queue. Do not use a state-level generalization as a national conclusion.

## Required fields for every state record

- State and any locality.
- Exact constitutional article, statutory section, or case citation.
- Date enacted, amended, or decided.
- Activity, office, person, or institution covered.
- Text-on-books status and operative enforceability.
- Historical evidence for religion connection.
- Competing causes and current legal rationale.

## Priority tracks

### 1. Religious-test provisions

- **Maryland:** *Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 (1961), invalidated Maryland’s belief-in-God requirement as applied to a notary public. Historic text and current enforceability must be distinct fields.
- **Texas:** Texas Constitution art. I, § 4 expressly bars a religious test for state office. Code as D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Other states:** Verify official constitutional text before coding surviving historic religious qualifications.

### 2. No-aid / Blaine provisions

- **Montana:** *Espinoza v. Montana Department of Revenue*, 591 U.S. 464 (2020), held that excluding religious schools from the scholarship program at issue violated the Free Exercise Clause.
- **Maine:** *Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. 767 (2022), held Maine could not exclude schools providing religious instruction from the tuition-assistance program at issue.
- **Method note:** Historical explanation may involve public-school policy, church-state theory, anti-Catholicism, and other causes. Do not assign a single motive without state-specific evidence.

### 3. Sunday / blue laws

Record by state and activity: vehicle sales, alcohol sales, hunting, retail business, recreation, licensing, or other restricted conduct.

- **Maine starter record:** Maine Legislature identifies 17 M.R.S. §§ 3203 and 3203-A as addressing business, traveling, recreation, penalties, relief, and exceptions related to Sunday activity. Verify each current subsection before a completed record.

### 4. Morality, family, and accommodation law

Build statute-by-statute records for contraception, abortion, divorce, adultery, same-sex conduct, obscenity, alcohol, gambling, marriage, religious accommodation, education, prisons, healthcare, and public services. Historical causation claims must be period-specific and jurisdiction-specific.
