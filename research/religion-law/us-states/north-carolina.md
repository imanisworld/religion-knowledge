# North Carolina — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** First-pass constitutional baseline verified; historical and statutory expansion pending  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26  
**Confidence:** High for cited constitutional text and federal enforceability rule

## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §13 — Religious liberty

North Carolina states that all persons have a natural and inalienable right to worship Almighty God according to conscience and that no human authority shall control or interfere with rights of conscience.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional protection of religious conscience.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article VI, §8 — Denial of God's existence as office disqualification

The current printed North Carolina Constitution still lists as an office disqualification any person who denies “the being of Almighty God.”

- **Legal text classification:** A — explicit religious qualification.
- **Current enforceability:** Unenforceable. Under *Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 (1961), a state may not constitutionally require belief in God as a qualification for public office.
- **Operative constitutional rule:** D at the federal level.
- **Confidence:** High.

This profile intentionally distinguishes text-on-books from enforceability.

## Education and public funding

Article IX, §1 states that “Religion, morality, and knowledge” are necessary to good government and the happiness of mankind and that schools, libraries, and education shall be encouraged. This is explicit religious language in the constitutional education article, but this first pass does not treat it as a standalone enforceable religious mandate.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

Article I, §13 provides the verified constitutional baseline. Additional statutory protections remain pending.

## Current religion-linked statutes

North Carolina statutes continue to prescribe oath/affirmation forms for public officers. A fuller statutory review remains pending.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

Article VI, §8's belief-in-God disqualification remains printed but is unenforceable under controlling federal constitutional law.

## Landmark cases

- *Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 (1961) — controlling federal rule against belief-in-God religious tests for public office.
