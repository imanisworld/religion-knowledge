## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §4 — Religious liberty

Utah protects rights of conscience, bars establishment and interference with free exercise, bars religious tests for public office and voting, bars witness or juror incompetence because of religious belief or its absence, bars union of church and state, and bars public money or property from being used for religious worship, exercise, instruction, or an ecclesiastical establishment.

- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article X, §§8–9 — Education

Article X, §8 bars religious or partisan tests as a condition of employment, admission, or attendance in the state's education systems. Section 9 bars state and local appropriations for the direct support of a school or educational institution controlled by a religious organization.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Utah Code §63G-33-201 — Free exercise of religion

Utah's statewide free-exercise statute provides that the free exercise of religion is a fundamental right applying to government action. Government may not substantially burden a person's free exercise, including through generally applicable rules, unless the burden is essential to a compelling governmental interest and is the least restrictive means. The statute provides claims or defenses and prevailing-party attorney fees and costs.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force; current cited text effective July 1, 2025.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and religion — 2026 enactment

### S.B. 268 — Religious Curriculum in Schools

Utah enacted S.B. 268 in 2026, effective July 4, 2026.

The act amended §53G-10-202 to permit academically framed study of religion and religion's influence, including comparative religion and the role of religion in history. It also provides that a public school may not sponsor or deny the practice of prayer or religious devotionals; officials may not use their positions to endorse, promote, or disparage religious, denominational, sectarian, agnostic, or atheistic viewpoints; and students' religious perspectives in assignments may not be discriminated against.

- **Classification:** D for the viewpoint-neutrality, student-expression, and nonendorsement protections.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

S.B. 268 also amended §53G-10-302. Instruction in American history and government now expressly includes study of the role of religion in U.S. history and religious liberty; the statutory list of historical documents includes the Ten Commandments; and public schools must display the national motto, “In God we trust,” in one or more prominent places in each school building.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-linked education requirements in enacted legal text.
- **Status:** In force as of July 4, 2026.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Causation note:** Classification A records explicit religious content in the law; it does not itself establish why the legislature enacted the provisions.

## Research caution

Utah's 2026 education provisions expressly regulate how religion is treated in curriculum and school practice. Recording that explicit text as category A does not establish that a religious doctrine was the historical cause of the enactment; any B or C causation claim requires separate legislative-history evidence.
