## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §18 — Freedom of worship, conscience, nonpreference, and public support

Wisconsin protects the right of every person to worship according to conscience, bars compelled attendance at or support of worship or ministry, bars control or interference with conscience, bars legal preference for religious establishments or modes of worship, and bars treasury money from being drawn for the benefit of religious societies or religious or theological seminaries.

- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article X, §3 — Sectarian instruction and released time

Wisconsin requires district schools to be free to resident children and provides that no sectarian instruction may be allowed in them. The Constitution also permits the Legislature to authorize release of students during regular school hours for religious instruction outside the district school.

- **No-sectarian-instruction rule:** D.
- **Released-time authorization:** A — explicit religion-specific constitutional authorization.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and religion

### Wis. Stat. §118.155 — Released time for religious instruction

Wisconsin permits a student, with written parental permission and under statutory conditions, to be excused from school for religious instruction outside the school. The statute sets limits on the amount of released time and places specified transportation and responsibility obligations outside the school district.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-specific accommodation.
- **Status:** In force in the current published statutes reviewed for this baseline.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Wis. Stat. §111.337 — Religious observance and practice in employment

Wisconsin's employment-discrimination law treats specified failures to reasonably accommodate religious observance or practice as creed discrimination unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. The law also contains defined exceptions involving religious associations and religion-related employment.

- **Classification:** D — protects religious observance and practice against specified employment burdens.
- **Status:** In force in the current published statutes reviewed for this baseline.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Pending constitutional amendment — not current law

### 2025–2026 A.J.R. 10 — Places of worship during emergencies

The Wisconsin Legislature completed second consideration of a proposed amendment to Article I, §18 concerning freedom to gather in places of worship during a state of emergency. The enrolled resolution was published May 26, 2026. The proposal is scheduled for the November 3, 2026 statewide ballot.

If ratified, the amendment would prohibit the state or a political subdivision from ordering the closure of, or forbidding gatherings in, places of worship in response to a state of emergency, including a public-health emergency.

- **Classification if enacted:** A — explicit religion-specific constitutional rule.
- **Current status:** **Proposed / pending voter ratification — not in force as of 2026-09-26.**

- **Confidence:** High as to pending status.
