# Washington — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** First-pass constitutional, statutory, and controlling federal case-law baseline verified; broader statutory and historical-causation expansion pending  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26  
**Confidence:** High for cited constitutional text, RCW provisions, and controlling U.S. Supreme Court law

## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §11 — Freedom of conscience, public support, and religious qualifications

Washington's Constitution protects absolute freedom of conscience in matters of religious sentiment, belief, and worship; bars disturbance because of religion; and prohibits public money or property from being appropriated for or applied to religious worship, exercise, instruction, or support of a religious establishment, subject to the text's chaplain exception. It also bars religious qualifications for public office or employment and protects witness and juror eligibility regardless of religious opinion.

- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force, with application subject to controlling federal constitutional doctrine.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article IX, §4 — Sectarian control or influence in publicly supported schools

All schools maintained or supported wholly or partly by public funds must be forever free from sectarian control or influence.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and religion

### RCW 28A.600.025 — Student religious expression

Washington public-school students retain rights of free speech and free exercise. When relevant to an assignment or activity, students may express or incorporate religious beliefs and may not receive a negative consequence solely because the expression is religious. School personnel may not impose their own religious beliefs on students.

- **Classification:** D — protects religious exercise and limits official religious coercion.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### RCW 28A.225.010 — Faith or conscience absences

Washington's compulsory-attendance statute permits an excused absence for up to two days per school year for reasons of faith or conscience or an organized activity conducted under the auspices of a religious denomination, church, or religious organization, with parental approval.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-specific legal accommodation.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### RCW 28A.642.010 — Religion and creed discrimination in public schools

Washington prohibits discrimination in public schools on enumerated grounds including religion and creed.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### RCW 48.43.065 — Health-care conscience and religious objections

Washington law provides specified conscience protections for health carriers, facilities, and providers, including provisions directed to religiously sponsored entities and objections based on conscience or religion, while also regulating enrollee access and notice.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion- and conscience-specific statutory accommodation.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High for the existence and current text; application is fact-specific.

## Landmark cases

### *Locke v. Davey*, 540 U.S. 712 (2004)

The U.S. Supreme Court upheld Washington's exclusion of devotional theology degrees from the scholarship program at issue against a federal Free Exercise challenge. The decision addressed a narrow funding context involving training for the ministry and does not create a general license to exclude religious participants from otherwise available public benefits.

- **Current relevance:** Article I, §11 public-funding rules operate within later federal Free Exercise doctrine as well as *Locke*'s narrower ministerial-training holding.

### *Kennedy v. Bremerton School District*, 597 U.S. 507 (2022)

The U.S. Supreme Court held that a Washington public school district violated a football coach's Free Exercise and Free Speech rights when it disciplined him for the personal religious observance at issue and rejected the district's Establishment Clause justification.

- **Current relevance:** Washington public-school rules must be applied consistently with federal Free Exercise, Free Speech, and Establishment Clause doctrine.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

Historical-causation review of Washington's no-aid and sectarian-school clauses remains pending. Their religious subject matter does not by itself establish a B or C historical-causation classification.

## Research caution

Washington's state constitutional language can be more textually restrictive than the federal Establishment Clause, but state restrictions cannot be applied in ways that violate controlling federal Free Exercise doctrine. Text, state-law interpretation, federal limits, and historical motive should remain separate fields.
