# Alaska — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** First-pass constitutional baseline verified; statutory expansion pending  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26  
**Confidence:** High for cited constitutional text

## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §4 — Freedom of Religion

Alaska's Constitution provides that no law shall be made respecting an establishment of religion or prohibiting the free exercise of religion.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional establishment and free-exercise protection.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### Article VII, §1 — Public Education

Article VII, §1 requires a statewide public-school system, requires schools and institutions established under the section to be free from sectarian control, and bars public funds from being paid for the **direct benefit** of any religious or other private educational institution.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional no-direct-benefit rule for religious and other private educational institutions.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting / public-funding rule.
- **Status:** In force; application depends on the meaning of "direct benefit" and controlling federal constitutional doctrine.
- **Confidence:** High.

The Alaska Legislature's constitutional guide describes the section as committing the state to public education without requiring absolute governmental indifference to students choosing private education, citing *Sheldon Jackson College v. State*, 599 P.2d 127 (Alaska 1979).

## Research caution

"No public funds for direct benefit" should not be collapsed into a claim that every neutral benefit reaching a private or religious-school student is constitutionally forbidden.
