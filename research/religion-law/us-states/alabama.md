## Constitutional provisions

### Alabama Religious Freedom Amendment — Constitution of Alabama of 2022, §3.01

Section 3.01 preserves the Alabama Religious Freedom Amendment. It provides that state and local government may not burden a person's freedom of religion, including through generally applicable rules, unless the government satisfies a compelling-interest / least-restrictive-means test. It also authorizes a person whose religious freedom is burdened in violation of the amendment to assert the violation as a claim or defense.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional protection for religious exercise against state and local government burdens.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting / religious-exercise protection.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Religious rights and liberties — Constitution of Alabama of 2022, §3.02

Section 3.02 protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled support of religious worship or ministers, and states that civil and political rights may not be diminished or enlarged because of religious belief. The same section also expressly addresses display of the Ten Commandments on state or public-school/public-body property, while requiring compliance with constitutional requirements.

- **Legal effect:** Mix of religious-liberty protections and an explicit constitutional provision concerning a named sacred/religious text.
- **Classification:** D for liberty/noncompulsion provisions; A for the explicit Ten Commandments display provision.
- **Status:** In force, subject to federal constitutional limits.
- **Confidence:** High.
