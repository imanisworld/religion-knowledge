## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §8 — Freedom of religion

Louisiana's 1974 Constitution provides: “No law shall be enacted respecting an establishment of religion or prohibiting the free exercise thereof.”

- **Status:** In force.
- **Legal effect:** State constitutional establishment and free-exercise protection using the First Amendment's familiar formulation.
- **Classification:** D — protects/limits religion.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### Balanced Treatment for Creation-Science and Evolution-Science Act

Louisiana R.S. 17:286.1–286.7 remains displayed in the official Revised Statutes even though it is unconstitutional.

- **Enacted:** Acts 1981, No. 685.
- **What it required:** Balanced treatment of “creation-science” and “evolution-science” when origins were taught, with additional curriculum-development provisions.
- **Controlling case:** *Edwards v. Aguillard*, 482 U.S. 578 (1987).
- **Official-code status:** Louisiana's current code pages expressly note that the Act was held unconstitutional in *Edwards*.
- **Historical causation:** The Supreme Court held that the statute advanced a religious doctrine and that the asserted “academic freedom” purpose did not supply a valid secular purpose for the mandate.
- **Classification:** Historical statute = A/B (explicit creation-science content with documented religious purpose); controlling constitutional rule = D.
- **Status:** Printed in code but unconstitutional / unenforceable.
- **Confidence:** High.

This record does not treat the unconstitutional Act as current Louisiana science curriculum policy.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Preservation of Religious Freedom Act — R.S. 13:5231 et seq.

Louisiana enacted the Preservation of Religious Freedom Act in 2010.

The current statutory scheme protects religious exercise against specified government burdens and provides a state-law cause of action and remedies. The Legislature amended portions of the Act in 2025.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Current religion-linked statutes

The Preservation of Religious Freedom Act is the principal general accommodation statute identified in this expansion pass. Additional healthcare, employment, and institution-specific conscience provisions remain for targeted review.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

The 1981 Balanced Treatment Act is the principal historical anchor: it remains visible in the code but is expressly flagged as unconstitutional.

## Landmark cases

- *Edwards v. Aguillard*, 482 U.S. 578 (1987) — invalidated Louisiana's creation-science mandate under the Establishment Clause.

## Historical-causation notes

For the creation-science statute, the evidence supports a direct causal classification because the statute's text, legislative design, and the Supreme Court's purpose analysis converge on a religiously grounded creationist objective. That conclusion is limited to the 1981 Act; it is not generalized to current Louisiana education law.
