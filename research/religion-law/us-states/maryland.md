# Maryland — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** Anchor baseline verified
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26

## Constitutional provisions

Maryland's current Declaration of Rights still prints belief-in-God language.

- **Article 36** protects religious liberty but also retains a proviso concerning belief in God for witness/juror competence.
- **Article 37** states that no religious test should be required for state office **other than a declaration of belief in the existence of God**.
- **Primary authority:** Maryland Declaration of Rights, arts. 36–37, current official Maryland General Assembly text.
- **Current enforceability:** The public-office belief requirement is unenforceable under *Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 (1961).
- **Classification:** Historical text = A (explicit religious qualification); controlling constitutional rule = D (religion-limiting rule).
- **Confidence:** High.

## Historical / unenforceable rule

*Torcaso* arose when Maryland refused a notary commission because the appointee would not declare belief in God. The U.S. Supreme Court held that requirement unconstitutional under the First and Fourteenth Amendments.

## Research caution

Text still printed in a constitution is not the same as an enforceable legal rule. This profile intentionally records both.
