# Michigan — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** First-pass constitutional baseline verified; education provision expressly marked limited by controlling state-court precedent  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26

## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §4 — Freedom of worship and religious belief; appropriations

Michigan guarantees worship according to conscience, bars compelled attendance or financial support of religious worship or religious teachers, bars state treasury appropriations or state-property use for the benefit of religious sects, societies, or theological/religious seminaries, and bars civil or political rights from being diminished or enlarged because of religious belief.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional conscience, noncompulsion, no-aid, and religious-equality protections.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### Article VIII, §2 — Public schools; nonpublic-school aid

Article VIII, §2 requires free public elementary and secondary schools and bars school-district discrimination based on religion, creed, race, color, or national origin. Its second paragraph contains a broad restriction on public aid to private, denominational, or other nonpublic pre-elementary, elementary, and secondary schools.

Michigan's Legislature expressly annotates the provision with the holding of *Traverse City School District v. Attorney General*, 384 Mich. 390, 185 N.W.2d 9 (1971): part of the second sentence of the second paragraph was held unconstitutional, void, and unenforceable under the federal Free Exercise and Equal Protection Clauses.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force, limited; the officially annotated unconstitutional portion is unenforceable.
- **Confidence:** High.
