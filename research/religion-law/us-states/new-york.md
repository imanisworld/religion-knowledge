## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §3 — Freedom of worship; religious liberty

New York allows free exercise and enjoyment of religious profession and worship without discrimination or preference and bars witness incompetence based on religious belief. The section also states that liberty of conscience does not excuse specified conduct inconsistent with peace or safety.

- **Legal effect:** State free-exercise, nonpreference, and witness protections.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### Article XI, §3 — Aid to denominational schools

New York bars state or subdivision property, credit, or public money from being used directly or indirectly in aid or maintenance of schools or institutions wholly or partly controlled by a religious denomination or teaching denominational doctrine, while expressly allowing transportation of children to and from schools.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional no-aid rule with an express transportation exception.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force, but application must be read alongside controlling federal Free Exercise doctrine.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Correction Law §610 — Freedom of worship

New York law guarantees free exercise and enjoyment of religious profession and worship without discrimination or preference to persons committed to specified correctional and reform institutions.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Current religion-linked statutes

- Correction Law §610 — institutional religious-exercise protection.
- Education Law §313 expressly preserves certain rights of religious or denominational educational institutions while regulating unfair educational practices.

## Research caution

New York's denominational-school funding restriction and its statutory protections for religious exercise answer different questions. Neither should be treated as a complete description of the state's church-state law.
