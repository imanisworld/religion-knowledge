# Virginia — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** First-pass constitutional and statutory baseline verified; case-law and historical-causation expansion pending  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26  
**Confidence:** High for cited current constitutional and statutory text

## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §16 — Free exercise; no establishment

Virginia protects free exercise according to conscience, bars compelled attendance at or support of worship and ministry, bars burdens because of religious opinions or belief, protects the right to profess and argue religious opinions without effect on civil capacities, bars religious tests, bars peculiar privileges for sects or denominations, and bars compulsory taxation for houses of worship or church ministry.

- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article IV, §16 — Appropriations to religious or charitable bodies

The General Assembly may not appropriate public funds, personal property, or real estate to a church or sectarian society or to an association or institution wholly or partly, directly or indirectly controlled by a church or sectarian society.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force as current constitutional text, subject to controlling federal constitutional doctrine and case law.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Virginia Act for Religious Freedom — Va. Code §§57-1 and 57-2

Virginia's code recites the 1786 Act for Religious Freedom and reaffirms the rights asserted there as natural and unalienable rights and as policy of the Commonwealth. The act bars compelled support of religious worship or ministry, protects profession and argument concerning religion, and states that religious opinion shall not affect civil capacities.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force as codified Commonwealth policy.
- **Confidence:** High.

The recited 1786 text expressly invokes God and religious concepts. That establishes explicit religious language in the primary text, but this profile does not convert that fact alone into a separate historical-causation claim about later Virginia law.

### Va. Code §57-2.02 — Religious freedom preserved

Virginia bars a government entity from substantially burdening a person's free exercise of religion, even through a generally applicable rule, unless the government demonstrates that the burden is essential to further a compelling governmental interest and is the least restrictive means. The statute provides a claim or defense and declaratory and injunctive relief, with attorney fees for a prevailing person; monetary damages are excluded.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force in the current Code of Virginia.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

Virginia is a useful example of a religious-liberty rule whose historical text is itself preserved inside the modern code. Historical-causation work should distinguish the 1786 enactment, later codification, later constitutional language, and present doctrine rather than treating them as a single event.

## Research caution

Virginia's constitutional no-aid language and statutory religious-freedom protections are current legal rules. Their present application to particular funding programs, institutions, or disputes requires program-specific case-law analysis rather than inference from the text alone.
