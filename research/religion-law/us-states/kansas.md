## Constitutional provisions

### Bill of Rights, §7 — Religious liberty; public office

Kansas protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled attendance or support of worship, bars interference with conscience and legal preference for a religious establishment or mode of worship, and bars religious tests for public office or voting and religious-belief disqualification of witnesses.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional conscience, noncompulsion, nonpreference, no-religious-test, and witness protections.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

Current Kansas statutes defining protected “exercise of religion” expressly cross-reference §7 of the Kansas Bill of Rights.

## Education and public funding

### K.S.A. 72-9930 — Sectarian doctrines; Holy Scriptures

Kansas law states that no sectarian or religious doctrines shall be taught or inculcated in a city's public schools, while providing that the section does not prohibit reading the Holy Scriptures without note or comment.

- **Legal effect:** Restricts sectarian/religious doctrinal teaching in covered public schools while expressly preserving a Bible-reading exception as written.
- **Classification:** D for the public-school religious-doctrine restriction; the explicit sacred-text exception is recorded as text but is not assigned a historical-causation code here.
- **Status:** In force in the 2026 Kansas Statutes.
- **Confidence:** High.

Kansas Constitution Article 6 also contains religion-related educational-funding language. Because a 2025–2026 proposed constitutional amendment targets that language, this first pass leaves the Article 6 funding entry for a dedicated current-status/history audit rather than mixing proposed and operative text.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Kansas Preservation of Religious Freedom Act — K.S.A. 60-5301 through 60-5305

K.S.A. 60-5303 bars government from substantially burdening a person's exercise of religion, including through a generally applicable rule, unless government proves by clear and convincing evidence that the burden furthers a compelling governmental interest through the least restrictive means. It authorizes claims, defenses, and specified judicial remedies.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force in the 2026 Kansas Statutes.
- **Confidence:** High.
