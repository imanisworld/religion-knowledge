## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §7 — Rights of conscience; education; religion and knowledge

Ohio protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled attendance or support of worship, bars legal preference for religious societies, protects rights of conscience, bars religious tests for office, and bars witness incompetence based on religious belief.

The same section also states that religion, morality, and knowledge are essential to good government and directs the General Assembly to protect every religious denomination in peaceful enjoyment of its mode of public worship and to encourage schools and instruction.

- **Legal effect:** Religious-liberty, noncompulsion, nonpreference, no-religious-test, and witness protections, together with explicit constitutional language assigning public importance to religion, morality, and knowledge.
- **Classification:** D for operative liberty/nonpreference/test protections. The explicit "religion, morality, and knowledge" language is recorded as textual context rather than treated here as a separate enforceable religious mandate.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Ohio Revised Code Chapter 3320 — student religious accommodations

Ohio law requires public-school policies providing full and reasonable accommodations for sincerely held religious beliefs and practices with respect to examinations, academic requirements, and absences for reasons of faith or religious or spiritual belief.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.
