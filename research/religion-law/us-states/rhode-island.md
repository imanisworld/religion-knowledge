## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §3 — Freedom of religion

Rhode Island's Constitution bars compelled attendance at or support of religious worship, bars burdens or civil incapacities because of religious belief, bars religious-belief disqualification from office, protects worship according to conscience, and states that religious opinion shall not alter a person's civil capacity.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional conscience, noncompulsion, religious-equality, and officeholding protection.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

Article I, §3 also contains an express historical statement describing Rhode Island's settlement as a “lively experiment” in religious liberty. That language is recorded as part of the primary constitutional text; this profile does not use it by itself to assign a broader historical-causation category to later Rhode Island laws.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### R.I. Gen. Laws §42-80.1-3 — Religious Freedom Restoration Act

Rhode Island bars a governmental authority from restricting free exercise of religion except where the restriction is a generally applicable rule that does not intentionally discriminate against religion or among religions and the government proves the restriction is essential to a compelling governmental interest and is the least restrictive means.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force in the current General Laws.
- **Confidence:** High.

### R.I. Gen. Laws §42-80.1-4 — Remedies

A court may award injunctive and declaratory relief against a governmental authority that violates or proposes to violate the chapter and may award a prevailing plaintiff damages.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.
