## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §4 — Liberty of conscience

California Constitution art. I, §4 guarantees free exercise and enjoyment of religion without discrimination or preference, bars establishment of religion by the Legislature, and bars religious belief from making a person incompetent as a witness or juror.

- **Legal effect:** State free-exercise, nonpreference, establishment, and witness/juror protections.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### Article IX, §8 — Sectarian schools and instruction

Article IX, §8 bars public money for sectarian or denominational schools or schools outside public-school control and bars sectarian or denominational doctrine in the common schools.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force, subject to controlling federal constitutional doctrine concerning neutral public-benefit programs.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article XVI, §5 — Public aid to religious entities

Article XVI, §5 restricts appropriations, grants, public funds, and specified donations in aid of religious sects, churches, creeds, sectarian purposes, and institutions controlled by religious bodies, subject to the section's stated exception.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force, with application constrained by federal constitutional doctrine where applicable.
- **Confidence:** High.

Historical motive for these no-aid provisions is not assigned in this pass.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Government Code §12940(l) — Employment accommodation

California's Fair Employment and Housing Act makes it an unlawful employment practice for a covered employer or entity to discriminate because an employment requirement conflicts with a person's religious belief or observance unless reasonable accommodation cannot be made without undue hardship. The section expressly includes Sabbath/holy-day observance and religious dress and grooming practices.

- **Legal effect:** Requires covered employers to reasonably accommodate religious belief/observance subject to statutory limits.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

Government Code §12926 also defines religious creed/religion broadly for the Act and excludes certain nonprofit religious associations or corporations from the Act's general "employer" definition.

## Current religion-linked statutes

## Research caution

California's no-aid language and its modern religious-accommodation rules answer different legal questions. They should not be merged into a single "pro-religion" or "anti-religion" characterization.
