## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, ¶3 — Conscience and compelled support

New Jersey protects worship according to conscience and bars compelling a person to attend a place of worship contrary to faith or judgment or to pay tithes, taxes, or other rates for churches, places of worship, or ministry contrary to the person's beliefs or voluntary commitments.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional protection of conscience and against compelled religious support.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, ¶4 — Establishment and religious tests

New Jersey bars establishment of one religious sect in preference to another and bars religious or racial tests for office or public trust.

- **Legal effect:** Nonpreference and no-religious-test rule.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, ¶5 — Civil and military rights

New Jersey bars denial or discrimination in civil or military rights, and segregation in the militia or public schools, because of religious principles, race, color, ancestry, or national origin.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

The current Constitution separately bars state/county/municipal donations or appropriations to societies, associations, or corporations generally. Because that provision is not religion-specific on its face, this first pass does not classify it as a religion-linked rule.

## Research caution

New Jersey's Constitution contains explicit worship, nonestablishment, and no-religious-test protections. Broader public-funding provisions should not be labeled religious merely because religious institutions can fall within their general scope.
