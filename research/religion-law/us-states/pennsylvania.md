## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §3 — Religious freedom

Pennsylvania protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled attendance or support of worship and ministry, bars human interference with rights of conscience, and bars legal preference for religious establishments or modes of worship.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional conscience, noncompulsion, and nonpreference protection.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, §4 — Religion and officeholding

The Constitution states that a person who acknowledges the being of a God and a future state of rewards and punishments shall not, because of religious sentiments, be disqualified from an office or place of trust or profit under the Commonwealth.

- **Legal effect:** Express religion-specific protection against office disqualification for the class described by the text.
- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-specific constitutional rule.
- **Status:** In force as constitutional text. This first pass does not infer from the clause that Pennsylvania may disqualify people outside the class it names.
- **Confidence:** High for the text; broader office-qualification doctrine remains a case-law expansion item.

## Education and public funding

### Article III, §15 — Public-school money and sectarian schools

Money raised for support of Pennsylvania public schools may not be appropriated to or used to support a sectarian school.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force, subject to controlling federal constitutional doctrine and current case law.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article III, §29 — Denominational and sectarian appropriations

Section 29 restricts appropriations for charitable, educational, or benevolent purposes to denominational and sectarian institutions and includes an exception structure for specified forms of assistance. Its scholarship language excludes grants or loans to persons enrolled in a theological seminary or school of theology.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force as constitutional text, subject to controlling federal constitutional doctrine and current case law.
- **Confidence:** High for the text.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Religious Freedom Protection Act — Act 214 of 2002

Section 4 bars an agency from substantially burdening a person's free exercise of religion, including through a generally applicable rule, unless the agency proves a compelling interest and the least restrictive means. Section 5 provides a claim or defense and sets procedural requirements and remedies.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.
