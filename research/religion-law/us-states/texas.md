# Texas — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** Two anchor issues verified; broader state profile pending
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26

## Constitutional religious-test language

Texas Constitution art. I, §4 states that no religious test shall be required for office or public trust, but retains a proviso that a person acknowledge “the existence of a Supreme Being.”

- **Primary authority:** Texas Constitution art. I, §4, current official state text.
- **Current enforceability:** A belief-in-God/Supreme-Being qualification for public office is incompatible with *Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 (1961).
- **Classification:** Printed text contains an explicit religious qualification (A); operative federal doctrine is D.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Sexual-conduct law

Texas Penal Code §21.06 still appears in the official code and criminalizes specified same-sex conduct.

- **Controlling case:** *Lawrence v. Texas*, 539 U.S. 558 (2003).
- **Official-code status note:** The Texas code itself states that §21.06 was declared unconstitutional by *Lawrence*.
- **Status:** Printed in code but unconstitutional / unenforceable.
- **Religion-causation classification:** Pending historical research. The subject matter alone is not enough to assign B or C.
- **Confidence:** High for text and enforceability.
