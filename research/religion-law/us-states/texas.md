## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §4 — Religious tests

Texas bars religious tests for office or public trust but still prints a proviso allowing officeholding protection only if a person acknowledges “the existence of a Supreme Being.”

- **Current enforceability:** A theistic office qualification is incompatible with *Torcaso v. Watkins*, 367 U.S. 488 (1961).
- **Classification:** Printed historical text = A; controlling constitutional rule = D.
- **Status:** Text remains; the religious qualification is unenforceable.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, §6 — Freedom of worship

Texas protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled support of worship or ministry, bars governmental interference with religious conscience, and bars legal preference for a religious society or mode of worship.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, §6-a — Religious services

Added in 2021, §6-a bars Texas or its political subdivisions from prohibiting or limiting religious services, including services conducted in churches, congregations, and places of worship.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, §7 — Sectarian appropriations

Texas bars appropriations of money or property for the benefit of a sect, religious society, theological or religious seminary, or similar religious use.

- **Status:** In force, subject to controlling federal Free Exercise doctrine where applicable.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Texas Religious Freedom Restoration Act — Civil Practice & Remedies Code Chapter 110

Texas enacted a general state RFRA in 1999.

Section 110.003 provides that a government agency may not substantially burden a person's free exercise of religion unless the burden furthers a compelling governmental interest through the least restrictive means. The chapter provides defenses and remedies and was amended in 2021 to add protections concerning closure of places of worship.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

This corrects the research handoff's statement that Texas had no state RFRA.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

### Penal Code §21.06 — “Homosexual Conduct”

Texas Penal Code §21.06 criminalized specified same-sex sexual conduct.

- **Enacted:** 1973 revision of the Texas Penal Code; effective January 1, 1974.
- **Controlling case:** *Lawrence v. Texas*, 539 U.S. 558 (2003).
- **Official-code status:** The current Texas code still prints §21.06 with a note that it was declared unconstitutional by *Lawrence*.
- **Status:** Unconstitutional / unenforceable.
- **Confidence:** High for text and legal status.

### Historical causation of §21.06

The broader American sodomy-law tradition has documented religious and moral-history roots, but the project distinguishes that long history from the **specific 1973 Texas decision** to create a same-sex-only offense while liberalizing treatment of opposite-sex conduct.

- **What is established:** *Lawrence* itself noted that earlier American sodomy laws generally were not directed at homosexuals as a distinct class.
- **What is not yet established:** A state-specific documentary transmission channel showing that identifiable religious organizations or religious arguments caused the 1973 Texas same-sex-only formulation.
- **Causation assessment:** **Broader tradition = mixed religious and secular moral influence; specific 1973 framing = unresolved.**
- **Confidence:** Medium for the broad history; low-to-medium for any stronger Texas-specific causal claim pending legislative records.
