## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §§2–5 — Worship, conscience, nonpreference, and religious tests

Indiana's Bill of Rights protects worship according to conscience, bars laws controlling the free exercise and enjoyment of religious opinions or interfering with conscience, bars legal preference for a creed, religious society, or mode of worship, bars compelled support of worship or ministry, and bars religious tests for offices of trust or profit.

- **Legal effect:** State constitutional religious-liberty, noncompulsion, nonpreference, and no-religious-test protections.
- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article I, §6 — Treasury aid

Section 6 states that no money shall be drawn from the treasury for the benefit of a religious or theological institution.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force, but not an absolute ban on every neutral public-benefit program involving religious schools.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

In *Meredith v. Pence* (Ind. 2013), the Indiana Supreme Court held that the school voucher program challenged there did not violate Article I, §4 or §6. The decision therefore matters to the operative scope of the no-preference and treasury-benefit clauses.

- **Status:** Constitutional clauses remain in force; their application is governed by state case law and federal constitutional doctrine.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Research caution

Indiana's constitutional text restricts religious preference and treasury benefit, but controlling state precedent rejects the claim that those clauses categorically prohibit the neutral voucher program at issue in *Meredith*.
