# Anchor-State Status

All eight priority anchor states now have **expanded verified profiles** rather than anchor-only baselines:

- **Maryland** — Articles 36–37, *Torcaso*, Sunday-law history, 1776 Christian-establishment context, current RFRA status, and 2026 worship-access legislation.
- **Arkansas** — Article 2 religion clauses, 2015 statutory RFRA, failed 2022 constitutional amendment, and the anti-evolution/*Epperson* history.
- **Louisiana** — Article I, §8, Preservation of Religious Freedom Act, and the unconstitutional Balanced Treatment Act/*Edwards* history.
- **Connecticut** — current constitutional religion provisions, §52-571b religious-exercise protection, and a documented but mixed-causation history for the 1879 contraception law.
- **Texas** — Articles I §§4, 6, 6-a, and 7, Chapter 110 state RFRA, and §21.06/*Lawrence* with the 1973 enactment-specific causation question left open.
- **Montana** — Article II §5, Article X §6, 2021 state RFRA, *Espinoza*, and a separated 1889 Blaine-era versus 1972 readoption causation analysis.
- **Maine** — Article I §3, *Carson* and the repealed nonsectarian tuition condition, current Sunday-sales statutes, release-time accommodation, and a deliberately narrow Sunday-law causation finding.
- **Massachusetts** — current religion and public-aid constitutional provisions, 1780 establishment and 1833 disestablishment history, and *Goodridge* with no direct religious-causation claim assigned to the inherited pre-*Goodridge* marriage exclusion.

## QA corrections made during expansion

The research handoff was treated as a source-discovery aid, not as authoritative text. Primary-source QA corrected several material points:

- Arkansas's 2022 Issue 3 **failed**; Arkansas instead has a statutory RFRA enacted in 2015.
- Connecticut has a RFRA-like statutory protection at Conn. Gen. Stat. §52-571b.
- Texas has a state RFRA in Civil Practice & Remedies Code Chapter 110.
- Montana enacted the Montana Religious Freedom Restoration Act in 2021.
- Maine's motor-vehicle Sunday restriction is part of an explicitly holy-day/Sunday statutory lineage, but the motive for the motor-vehicle-specific enactment remains unresolved.
- Massachusetts's 1833 Amendment XI is historically important but was later superseded; current religion/public-aid rules come from later constitutional amendments.

## Remaining targeted gaps

These profiles are materially broader than the original anchors but are **not exhaustive state legal encyclopedias**. Remaining work is concentrated in:

- religious-property tax exemptions;
- healthcare and employment conscience/accommodation statutes;
- some Sunday/blue-law histories;
- program-specific education funding/curriculum disputes;
- Texas's 1973 Penal Code legislative record for §21.06;
- Montana's 1972 convention motives for retaining the no-aid clause;
- Maine's enactment-specific history for the current motor-vehicle Sunday rule.

A landmark case or a current statutory text does not by itself establish historical causation. The project continues to keep **current legal effect**, **historical origin**, and **documented causal transmission** as separate fields.
