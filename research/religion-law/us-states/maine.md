# Maine — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** Expanded anchor profile verified; §3203 enactment history reviewed (gap closed 2026-10-20)  
**Last verified:** 2026-10-20 (§3203 history); 2026-09-27 (all other sections)  
**Confidence:** High for current statutes, constitutional text, and *Carson*; medium for historical causation of the motor-vehicle-specific Sunday rule

## Constitutional provisions

### Article I, §3 — Religious freedom

Maine protects worship according to conscience and bars injury, restraint, or civil disability based on religious profession or sentiment, subject to public-peace limits.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D — protects/limits religion.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### Tuition assistance and *Carson v. Makin*

Maine formerly required a participating private school to be “nonsectarian” to receive tuition-assistance payments under 20-A M.R.S. §2951(2).

- **Controlling case:** *Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. 767 (2022).
- **Holding:** Maine could not exclude otherwise eligible schools from the tuition program because they provided religious instruction.
- **Current statutory status:** The former “Nonsectarian” subsection was repealed by P.L. 2025, c. 112, §5; the official current §2951 page shows subsection 2 repealed.
- **Classification:** Historical funding restriction = D; controlling federal Free Exercise rule = D.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Release time for religious observance

Maine law permits local school units to grant public-school students up to one hour per week of release time for moral instruction or religious observance away from school, subject to local policy and no added cost to the school unit.

- **Citation:** 20 M.R.S. §1224-A.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D — accommodation of religious exercise.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

This pass found **no enacted general Maine RFRA** comparable to Texas, Louisiana, Arkansas, or Montana.

A 2015 proposal, L.D. 1340, would have enacted a “Preservation of Religious Freedom Act,” but the Legislature records its disposition as **Leave to Withdraw**.

- **Current general baseline:** Maine Constitution art. I, §3 plus federal constitutional protections.
- **Confidence:** High that L.D. 1340 did not become law; issue-specific accommodations remain open.

## Current religion-linked statutes

### 17 M.R.S. §3203 — Sunday motor-vehicle sales

Maine currently prohibits specified buying, selling, exchanging, dealing, or trading in new or used motor vehicles on Sunday, subject to §3203-A.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Penalty:** Class E crime.
- **Enactment:** S.P. 381 / L.D. 1107, “An Act to Prohibit the Selling of Motor Vehicles on Sunday,” 99th Legislature (1959).
- **Who asked for it:** The Maine Automobile Dealers Association. In the Senate, Sen. Dow said the bill “was sponsored by the Maine Automobile Dealers Association” and that “80 per cent voted to close on Sunday,” and Sen. Hillman said he sponsored it “in behalf of the automobile dealers” ([Senate Legislative Record, May 5, 1959, pp. 1363–1365](https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/LegRec/_99/Senate/LegRec_1959-05-05_SP_p1359-1367.pdf)). Dow, opposing, called it “discriminatory.”
- **Stated reasons:** In the House, Mr. Good of Sebago said the industry “requested that they be permitted to observe the Lord's Day”; the dealers' questionnaire showed 77% in favor; and Rep. Dumais said it was “impossible to finance a sale on a Sunday” and that Sunday visitors were mostly “curiosity seekers.” The committee vote was 9–1 ([House Legislative Record, May 6, 1959, p. 1420](https://lldc.mainelegislature.org/Open/LegRec/_99/House/LegRec_1959-05-06_HP_p1419-1453.pdf)).
- **Classification:** **C — Mixed religious influence.** Industry and competitive motives predominate, with explicit Lord's Day framing in the debate and placement in the “Holy Days” subchapter. No separate religious-organization lobbying was found. Structured record: `US-ME-17-3203`. Full analysis: `../gap-closure/maine-sunday-motor-vehicle-rule.md`.
- **Confidence:** High for current effect; medium for historical-causation classification.

### Broader Sunday-law chapter

Section 3203 sits in Title 17, Chapter 105, “Religious Assemblies, Holy Days and Holidays,” Subchapter 1, “Holy Days.” Section 3201 still defines Sunday as the “Lord's Day,” and §3204 governs business, travel, and recreation on Sunday with numerous exceptions.

That statutory architecture supplies direct evidence that Maine's Sunday rules descend from a Sabbatarian/holy-day legal tradition. It does **not**, by itself, prove that the modern motor-vehicle-specific restriction was enacted for an expressly religious purpose.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

Maine's chapter retains multiple repealed Sunday-law provisions alongside the current sections. The surviving framework reflects a legal transition from explicitly “Lord's Day” regulation toward modern commercial and day-of-rest rules.

- **Causation assessment:** **Documented religious historical influence** for the broader Sunday-law lineage; **mixed causation, industry-driven with explicit Lord's Day framing,** for the 1959 motor-vehicle-sales restriction (see §3203 above).
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

This is deliberately narrower than the research handoff's claim that Puritan Sabbatarianism directly caused the current §3203 without an enactment-specific legislative record. The 1959 record shows the dealers, not a religious body, supplied the organized pressure.

## Landmark cases

- *Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. 767 (2022) — invalidated Maine's exclusion of religious instruction from the tuition-assistance program at issue.
