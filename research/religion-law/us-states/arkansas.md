# Arkansas — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** Expanded anchor profile verified; targeted historical and accommodation gaps remain  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-27  
**Confidence:** High for constitutional text, RFRA status, and *Epperson*; medium for broader historical causation outside the anti-evolution law

## Constitutional provisions

### Article 2, §24 — Religious liberty

Arkansas protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled support of worship or ministry, bars governmental interference with conscience, and bars legal preference for a religious establishment, denomination, or mode of worship.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D — protects/limits religion.
- **Confidence:** High.

### Article 2, §26 — Religious tests

Arkansas bars religious tests for voting or public office and bars witness incompetence based on religious belief.

- **Status:** In force.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### 1928 anti-evolution initiative

Arkansas Initiated Act No. 1 of 1928 prohibited teaching in state-supported schools or universities that humans descended from a lower order of animals and restricted use of textbooks teaching that theory.

- **Controlling case:** *Epperson v. Arkansas*, 393 U.S. 97 (1968).
- **Legal effect:** Unconstitutional under the Establishment Clause.
- **Historical causation:** The Supreme Court described the law as a product of the fundamentalist religious movement of the 1920s and held that the State could not tailor public-school teaching to the principles of a religious sect.
- **Classification:** Historical enactment = B/A because the restriction was religiously motivated and directly targeted a scientific account conflicting with a biblical creation view; controlling constitutional rule = D.
- **Status:** Historical / unenforceable.
- **Confidence:** High.

This classification is confined to the 1928 enactment. It does not imply that current Arkansas science standards share the same origin.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### Arkansas Religious Freedom Restoration Act — Act 975 of 2015

Arkansas enacted a general state RFRA in 2015, codified at Ark. Code §§16-123-401 et seq.

The Act provides that government may not substantially burden a person's exercise of religion, including through a rule of general applicability, unless government proves that the burden furthers a compelling governmental interest through the least restrictive means. The statutory scheme also provides claims, defenses, and remedies.

- **Status:** In force; amended in 2023.
- **Classification:** D.
- **Confidence:** High.

### 2022 Issue 3 — proposed constitutional amendment

A proposed constitutional religious-freedom amendment appeared on the 2022 general-election ballot.

- **Result:** It **did not pass**. Official results show 433,475 votes for and 440,687 against.
- **Effect:** Arkansas therefore has the 2015 statutory RFRA, not a 2022 constitutional RFRA.
- **Confidence:** High.
