# Vermont — Religion & Law Profile

**Research status:** First-pass constitutional and education baseline verified; broader statutory and case-law expansion pending  
**Last verified:** 2026-09-26  
**Confidence:** High for cited constitutional text and published state statutory text; moderate-high for the current tuition-program status summary

## Constitutional provisions

### Chapter I, Article 3 — Freedom in religion

Vermont's Constitution protects worship according to conscience, bars compelled attendance at or support of religious worship and ministry contrary to conscience, bars civil-rights deprivation because of religious sentiments or modes of worship, and bars governmental control of the rights of conscience in the free exercise of religious worship.

- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

Article 3 also ends with a sentence stating that every sect or denomination of Christians “ought to observe the sabbath or Lord's day” and maintain some form of religious worship agreeable to the revealed will of God.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religious constitutional text.
- **Status:** Still present in the current Constitution.
- **Legal-effect caution:** The language is phrased as “ought,” not as an independently specified criminal or civil command. This first pass does not assign an enforcement mechanism that the text itself does not provide.
- **Confidence:** High for the text.

### Chapter II, §68 — Religious societies

Vermont's Constitution states that religious societies and bodies united or incorporated for advancement of religion and learning, or other pious and charitable purposes, shall be encouraged and protected in the privileges, immunities, and estates they ought to enjoy under regulations directed by the General Assembly.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-specific constitutional text.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and public funding

### 16 V.S.A. ch. 24 — Periodic released time for religious education

Vermont states a policy of cooperating with religious groups by adjusting public-school schedules to permit periodic released time for religious instruction, so long as secular educational programs are not disrupted. The religious instruction may not be conducted on public-school property, school personnel may not direct students to participate or not participate, parental or guardian permission is required, and the sponsoring religious group bears the costs.

- **Classification:** A for the explicit religion-specific released-time framework; D for the safeguards limiting public-school religious direction and expenditure.
- **Published status:** Present in the Vermont Statutes Online.
- **Confidence:** High for the published text.

### Religious-school tuition and Article 3

Vermont legislative materials describe *Chittenden Town School District v. Department of Education* (Vt. 1999) as holding that public tuition may go to a school with a religious mission only with safeguards against use of the funds for religious worship, instruction, or propagation of religious views.

The State's later implementation is constrained by federal Free Exercise doctrine. A Vermont legislative research document records that, after *Carson v. Makin* and settlements in *E.W. v. French* and *A.H. v. French*, the Agency of Education advised school districts in 2022 that they may not deny tuition payments to religious approved independent schools solely on the basis of Vermont's Compelled Support Clause; districts were instructed to consider both *Carson* and *Chittenden*.

- **Status:** State compelled-support doctrine remains relevant, but categorical exclusion based solely on religious status is constrained by controlling federal constitutional law.
- **Confidence:** Moderate-high for this first-pass current-status description.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### 16 V.S.A. §134 — Religious exemption from specified health instruction

The published statute exempts a student from instruction concerning disease, symptoms, development, and treatment when a parent submits a signed statement that the instruction conflicts with the parent's religious convictions; the student may not be penalized for the exemption.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-specific statutory accommodation.
- **Published status:** Present in the Vermont Statutes Online.
- **Confidence:** High for the published text.

## Historical or unenforceable rules

The Christian Sabbath sentence in Article 3 is current constitutional text. Its historical development and practical legal effect should be researched separately rather than inferred from its presence alone.

## Landmark cases

- *Chittenden Town School District v. Department of Education*, 738 A.2d 539 (Vt. 1999) — state compelled-support decision concerning tuition at a religious school, as summarized in Vermont legislative materials.
- *Carson v. Makin*, 596 U.S. 767 (2022) — federal Free Exercise precedent affecting religious-school exclusion from generally available tuition programs.
