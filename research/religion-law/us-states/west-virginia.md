# West Virginia — Religion & Law Profile

**Last verified:** 2026-09-26

## Constitutional provisions

### Article III, §15 — Religious freedom

West Virginia protects profession and argument concerning religion, bars compelled attendance at or support of worship or ministry, bars burdens on civil capacities because of religious opinions or belief, bars religious tests, bars peculiar privileges for religious sects or denominations, and bars compulsory taxation for houses of worship or church ministry.

- **Classification:** D — religion-limiting rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Religious accommodation and liberty

### W. Va. Code §35-1A-1 et seq. — Equal Protection for Religion Act

West Virginia's Equal Protection for Religion Act bars state action from substantially burdening a person's exercise of religion unless the action is the least restrictive means of furthering a compelling governmental interest. It also bars treating religious conduct more restrictively than reasonably comparable nonreligious conduct under specified standards.

The Act supplies a claim or defense and authorizes specified declaratory, injunctive, and fee remedies.

- **Classification:** D.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

## Education and religion

### W. Va. Code §18-33-2 et seq. — Student Religious Liberties Act

West Virginia public schools may not discriminate against students or parents on the basis of a religious viewpoint or religious expression. The Act addresses religious expression in assignments, voluntary prayer and religious activity, student religious organizations, and religious messages or symbols, while preserving academic requirements and constitutional limits.

- **Classification:** D — protects private religious expression and limits school discrimination or coercion.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.

### W. Va. Code §18-2-9a — Elective courses on the Bible

West Virginia permits public high schools to offer elective social-studies courses concerning the Hebrew Scriptures or Old Testament, the New Testament, or both. The statute directs academic study of their literary, historical, and cultural influence and requires religious neutrality rather than endorsement, favor, disfavor, or hostility.

- **Classification:** A — explicit religion-linked education rule.
- **Status:** In force.
- **Confidence:** High.
- **Causation note:** Classification A describes the statute's explicit religious subject matter; it does not establish the historical cause of enactment.

## Research caution

A statute directed expressly to religion can be category A because religion appears in the legal rule while still functioning as a protection for religious exercise. The classification describes the legal relationship being recorded; it is not a claim about legislative motive.
