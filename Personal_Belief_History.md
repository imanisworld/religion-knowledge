# Personal Belief History — Reconstruction Log

*Personal reflection companion. This is not part of the research corpus and is not evidence for or against any religious claim.*

## Purpose

Reconstruct what I actually believed at different stages of my life without rewriting the past from my current perspective.

The governing rule is simple: **unknown stays unknown**. Do not assign earlier beliefs to me merely because I was baptized, attended church, used Christian language, or later accepted or rejected a doctrine.

## What is currently established

- I was baptized at about **16**. This is based on my present recollection; no contemporaneous record has been checked yet.
- I do **not** clearly remember how I understood Christianity during adolescence or college.
- My current framing of wrongdoing is closer to **“I do things that are wrong sometimes”** than **“I am a sinner”** as a permanent identity or human condition.
- That current framing must **not** be projected backward onto teenage or college me without evidence.

## Evidence rules

Use the strongest available evidence in this order:

1. **Contemporaneous evidence** — journals, messages, emails, social posts, school writing, church/baptism material, notes, recordings.
2. **Near-contemporaneous evidence** — something written or said close to the period being reconstructed.
3. **Present recollection** — useful, but vulnerable to memory distortion and later reinterpretation.
4. **Inference** — permitted only when clearly labeled; never convert it into a remembered fact.

Participation is not doctrine. Baptism, church attendance, prayer, or calling myself Christian may show religious identification or practice, but none of those facts by themselves establish what I believed about a specific doctrine.

## Reconstruction timeline

|---|---|---|

## Questions to reconstruct rather than assume

For each period, recover evidence for what I actually thought about:

- God: personal being, creator, intervention, prayer, miracles.
- Jesus: divinity, resurrection, salvation, historical person, relationship to God.
- Trinity and Holy Spirit.
- The Bible: literal, inspired, inerrant, authoritative, symbolic, historical, or something else.
- Sin: actions versus identity; original sin; guilt; human nature.
- Salvation and atonement: what I thought people needed saving from and why.
- Heaven, hell, judgment, Satan, demons, afterlife.
- Morality: whether right and wrong depended on God or could stand independently.
- Science and history: evolution, creation, miracles, biblical historicity.
- Gender, sexuality, marriage, family roles.
- Church, clergy, denominations, religious authority.
- Doubts: what bothered me, when, and whether I voiced it at the time.

## Evidence to look for

Potential sources include old journals, school papers, notes apps, text messages, DMs, emails, social-media posts, church or baptism records, old Bible annotations, photos/captions, and conversations preserved in writing.

When evidence is found, record the date, source, exact claim, confidence level, and whether it reflects belief, uncertainty, social conformity, or merely language I was using.

## Do not backfill the story

The point of this page is not to create a neat deconversion or belief narrative after the fact. It is to preserve the difference between:

- what I **know** I believed,
- what I **currently remember** believing,
- what the evidence **suggests**,
- and what I simply **do not know**.

A gap is better than a false memory.
